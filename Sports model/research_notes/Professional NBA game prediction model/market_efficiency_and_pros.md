# Market efficiency and professional practice: the bar a new NBA model must clear

Compiled 19 November 2026 for a one-person Python NBA model (free data, runs on a Mac) that predicts score, margin, total and win probability and compares them with DraftKings and Fanatics prices.

Reading guide
- Every number carries its season range and sample size (n) where the source gives one.
- [pre-2018] = evidence older than the preferred 2018-19+ window, kept only where nothing newer was found.
- [snippet] = taken from a search-engine summary because the page itself could not be opened (HTTP 403/404). Lower confidence.
- [dated?] = may be out of date as of November 2026.

- The web-search allowance ran out before every lead was followed; the Gaps lists say what was left open.

## 1. How efficient are NBA closing lines? (spreads, totals, moneylines; opening versus closing; sharp versus retail books; limits and timing)

### Takeaway
The betting line is the best public forecast of an NBA game. It misses the final margin by roughly 9 to 10.5 points on average (root-mean-square error about 12 to 13), misses totals by a root-mean-square error of about 18 points, names about 68-69% of winners, and shows no bias large enough to beat the bookmaker's margin in samples of 20,000+ games. Lines move between open and close in more than 80% of games and the close is the more accurate number; DraftKings and Fanatics mostly copy market-making books (Pinnacle, Circa), charge a higher margin (about 4.9-5.1% against 3.8%) and limit customers who beat the close.

### Cited Findings

#### Spread and winner accuracy
- Las Vegas opening spreads, scored out of sample on the second half of each regular season from 2006-07 to 2013-14 (8 seasons; the first ~615 games of each season were the fitting window): mean squared error 137.49 (RMSE 11.7 points), mean absolute error 9.17 points, correct winner 69.2%. The best team-strength model scored MSE 142.21, MAE 9.36, 68.2% winners, and was statistically worse than the spread (excluded from the 95% model confidence set). Playoffs: spread MAE 9.50, MSE 148.96, 68.0% winners. [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016, Journal of Quantitative Analysis in Sports, author PDF](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- The same paper puts the in-sample residual variance of game margins at about 130 against the spread's out-of-sample MSE of about 137.5, and concludes there is very little room left for improvement and that about 69% of winners is close to the practical ceiling. [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- A hobby project covering about 23,000 games from 2006 to 2026 reports the average miss of the Vegas spread as 9.12 points in 2006-2016 and 10.49 points in 2020-2026 (regular season about 9.64, postseason about 10.18), and attributes the rise to more three-point shooting and game-to-game variance. The source of its historical lines is not named and it does not say whether they are closing lines; repository archived 1 November 2026. — [NBA-Betting/NBA_Betting on GitHub](https://github.com/NBA-Betting/NBA_Betting)
- Picking the bookmaker's favourite was right 69% (plus or minus 2.5 points by season) across 9,093 games, seasons 2006-2014 (Pinnacle closing odds for 2010-2014, other bookmakers before that). [pre-2018] — [Hubáček PhD thesis 2024, chapter 19 (reprints the 2019 International Journal of Forecasting study)](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- Opening favourites covered the closing spread in about 50.0% of roughly 22,500 NBA games with both an opening and a closing spread (11,081 covers, 11,100 non-covers, 361 pushes; totals are sums of the page's tables; the page says its data runs through the end of 2025-26 and a search summary dates the start to the 2008 season). — [TeamRankings, ATS results by line movement](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/ats/movement/)
- At DraftKings in 2025-26 (1,322 games tracked), the side with the majority of money went 653-652 against the spread (50.0%, ROI -4.9%) and the side with the majority of tickets went 625-652 (48.9%, ROI -7.2%). — [VSiN, NBA betting splits strategies for 2026-27](https://vsin.com/nba/nba-betting-splits-strategies-for-the-2026-2027-season/)

#### Totals
- Across 10,320 games from 2000-01 to 2007-08 the average posted total was 192.27 and the average final total 192.72; overs 5,059, unders 5,086, exact landings 175 (1.7%). [pre-2018] — [Crown Wagers summary of Compton and Sigler, The Sport Journal 2012](https://www.crownwagers.com/nba-deviation-for-game-totals/); original at [The Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/nba-gambling-inefficiencies-a-second-look/) (returned 403, not opened)
- The "Vegas" total had a mean squared error of 320.98 in 2021-22 and 332.08 in 2022-23, i.e. an RMSE of about 17.9 to 18.2 points (about 1,200 games per season; student project; the book and whether the line is opening or closing are not stated). — [Carnegie Mellon statistics capstone, spring 2024](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2024/460files/team6.pdf)
- Forum posters put the bookmaker's mean absolute error on totals at about 13 to 13.4 points (December 2024; anecdotal, no data shown). — [r/algobetting thread](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/algobetting/comments/1hbezkg/nba_total_score_predicting)
- Against the closing total, 23,179 games split 11,502 over, 11,308 under, 369 pushes (50.4% over). A search summary of the same site reports that against the opening total 19,975 games since 2008 went 9,805 over, 9,905 under, 265 pushes (49.7% over). — [TeamRankings, over/under by line movement](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/over-under/over/)
- At DraftKings in 2025-26 the majority-money side on totals went 650-643 (50.3%, ROI -4.4%) and the majority-ticket side 636-667 (48.8%, ROI -7.5%). — [VSiN](https://vsin.com/nba/nba-betting-splits-strategies-for-the-2026-2027-season/)

#### Moneylines
- 3,670 NBA games, 2022-23 to 2024-25, about ten licensed US sportsbooks per bet (The-Odds-API data, 34,944 quotes per timestamp): the average margin built into moneyline odds was 4.5%, and the author finds no favourite-longshot bias in NBA moneylines. — [Karl Whelan, The Truth about Closing Line Value, 12 August 2026](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)
- At DraftKings in 2025-26, backing the majority-money side on the moneyline won 847 of 1,305 bets (64.8%) and still returned -10.1%; over three seasons the majority-money home moneyline went 1,722-762 for -12.3%. — [VSiN](https://vsin.com/nba/nba-betting-splits-strategies-for-the-2026-2027-season/)
- The bookmaker's margin is not spread evenly: in Pinnacle's 2010-2014 NBA odds it grows sharply on heavy favourites (odds near 1.1). [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis, section 19.2](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- One documented break in efficiency: during the COVID seasons, backing every NBA moneyline underdog returned 16.7%, concentrated in underdogs priced from +233 to +400; the authors suspect empty arenas and found nothing similar in other sports. — [Qureshi and Zaman, arXiv 2109.07581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07581)

#### Opening versus closing lines
- How far spreads move (about 22,500 games, same TeamRankings sample): no change in 3,864 games (17%), 0.5 to 1.5 points in 12,911 (57%), 2 to 2.5 points in 2,950 (13%), 3 or more points in 2,817 (12.5%). — [TeamRankings](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/ats/movement/)
- After the move the closing spread is unbiased: opening favourites covered the close 50.1% when the line moved 0.5 to 1.5 points toward them (n = 7,110), 50.9% after a 2 to 2.5 point move toward them (n = 1,628), 48.6% after 3+ toward them (n = 1,071), 50.4% after 0.5 to 1.5 toward the underdog (n = 5,801), 47.8% after 2 to 2.5 toward the underdog (n = 1,322) and 48.3% after 3+ toward the underdog (n = 1,746). — [TeamRankings](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/ats/movement/)
- How far totals move (23,179 games): no change in 1,981 (8.5%), 0.5 to 1.5 points in 9,341 (40%), 2 to 2.5 in 4,418 (19%), 3 or more in 7,439 (32%). Against the closing total, overs hit 49.9% when the total had moved up (n = 10,255), 51.1% when it had moved down (n = 10,943) and 49.5% with no move. (Bucket sums are from the page's tables.) — [BetIQ / TeamRankings, over-under by line movement and opening line](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/nba/betting-trends/over-under-results-by-line-movement-and-opening-line/)
- Closing NBA totals are more accurate forecasts of points scored than opening totals, and line changes go in the right direction and by about the right amount to remove biases in the openers. [pre-2018; sample not shown on the abstract page] — [Gandar, Zuber and Dare 2000, Journal of Sports Economics](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v1y2000i2p177-186.html)
- Movements of NBA point spreads from open to close show that informed bettors drive the price. [pre-2018] — [Gandar, Dare, Brown and Zuber 1998, Journal of Finance](https://econbiz.de/Record/informed-traders-and-price-variations-in-the-betting-market-for-professional-basketball-games-gandar-john/10005214276)
- Moneyline quotes taken 24 hours before tip-off are far more dispersed against the close than later quotes: the best tenth earned about 16% and the worst tenth lost about 25% (3,670 games, 2022-23 to 2024-25). — [Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)

#### Sharp books versus US retail books
- Median hold on closing main lines, 12-18 October 2026, ten leagues combined (NBA was in preseason, so not broken out): Pinnacle 3.79% (1,654 markets), Circa 4.58% (136), DraftKings 4.93% (1,070), FanDuel 4.96% (1,066), BetMGM 5.03% (1,168), Fanatics 5.11% (784), Caesars 5.67% (896). A -110/-110 market holds 4.76%. One week of data. — [SharpAPI Sportsbook Hold Report, issue 1](https://sharpapi.io/research/hold-report)
- Recreational books such as DraftKings and BetMGM mostly copy the prices of market-making books (Circa, Pinnacle) rather than discovering prices themselves; market-makers offer far fewer markets but take bigger bets from anyone. — [Jack Miller, Establish The Run, 13 April 2024](https://establishtherun.com/?p=812308)
- Market-makers named as Pinnacle, Betcris and the Betfair exchange; Vegas-style books such as Circa post their own number at low limits and let sharp bettors shape it; most other books follow. — [Ben Porter, Where do odds come from?, 18 July 2022](https://benporter.substack.com/p/where-do-odds-come-from)
- Sportsbooks do not run balanced books; they take a position on nearly every game and rely on the margin over many bets. — [Karl Whelan, The Balanced Book Myth](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2573)

#### Limits and timing
- Pinnacle: NBA games generally posted by 14:00 Pacific the day before; opening limits $5,000 on spreads and $2,000 on totals; raised in steps until about 07:00 Pacific on game day; full limits $20,000 spread, $10,000 moneyline, $5,000 total. [snippet] [dated?] — [Pinnacle help centre](https://pinnaclesports.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/1000115292-when-are-nba-limits-and-odds-available-)
- NBA lines usually appear the night before or the morning of the game; books open the next day's games in the evening at low limits and raise them late the following morning; a game with a star listed as questionable may stay off the board or open late. — [BettorEdge, 16 October 2026](https://bettoredge.com/post/when-do-betting-lines-come-out)
- Circa's sportsbook director has said that opening at $2,000-3,000 limits is too small to draw out the sharpest bettors' information, and Circa is known for not limiting winners. [snippet; context may not be NBA] — [Legal Sports Report Q&A](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/?p=53006)
- Books open at low limits so that sharp bettors push the number first; limits rise as the early value disappears. One professional watches an Asian book for NBA lines because he expects that market to move first. [2019] — [Covers interview with Gadoon "Spanky" Kyrollos](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor)
- NBA injury reports for 2026-27: due by 5 p.m. local time the day before a game; by 1 p.m. on game day for the second night of a back-to-back; a game-day report is due between 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. local (8 to 10 a.m. for tips at 5 p.m. or earlier), except on the second night of a back-to-back. The league's archive shows reports published every 15 minutes. — [NBA official injury report page, 2026-27](https://official.nba.com/nba-injury-rep)
- Before December 2025 the public report refreshed hourly; the league moved to 15-minute refreshes to shrink the window in which late injury information can be bet. [snippet] — [Bleacher Report on ESPN reporting, December 2025](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25334094-nba-reportedly-enacts-sports-betting-policies-around-injury-reporting-more)
- Size of injury moves, per oddsmakers during the 2026 playoffs: Jokic 5.5 points of spread, Gilgeous-Alexander 4, Wembanyama 4, Doncic 3.5, Cunningham and Edwards 3, second-tier stars 2 to 2.5, most other starters 1 or less. San Antonio was a 1.5-point favourite without Wembanyama and a 5.5-point favourite with him one game later. — [theScore](https://www.thescore.com/nba/news/3531319/nba-playoff-injury-woes-how-much-are-stars-worth-to-their-teams)
- A newly found edge lasts about three days before bookmakers adjust, according to Nate Silver's account of Haralabos Voulgaris. [pre-2018] — [Sports Illustrated interview with Nate Silver, 2012](https://www.si.com/more-sports/2012/09/12/nate-silver)

### Inferences
- Targets for the builder's test harness, on the same games: spread MAE about 9.2 (2006-14) rising to about 10.5 (2020-26); spread RMSE about 11.7 then, about 13 now (10.49 x 1.25 if errors are near normal); total RMSE about 18; winner accuracy 68-69%. A model that lands within about 0.2 MAE of the line has matched the best published ratings models and is still not profitable.
- Published accuracy numbers are era-dependent. The line's own error rose by about 15% between 2006-16 and 2020-26, so a model must be compared with the line on the same games, never with a number from another paper.
- The closing line being unbiased after moves of 2+ points means the opener was wrong by roughly the size of the move on those games. A quarter of spreads and half of totals move 2+ points, so the realistic source of value for a small bettor is being early and right about which way the number will go, not out-predicting the close.
- In the TeamRankings data, when the spread moved 2+ points toward the underdog the underdog still covered the close 51.9% (1,564-1,447, n about 3,000). That is about two standard errors above 50% and below the 52.4% break-even, so it is a hint of slight under-reaction at most, not an edge.
- Retail prices are a noisier, more expensive copy of the sharp price. For DraftKings and Fanatics the extra margin over Pinnacle is about 1.1 to 1.3 percentage points of hold, which a bettor must overcome in addition to finding an edge.
- One point of spread is worth about 3.0 to 3.4 percentage points of cover probability (normal approximation with an SD of 11.7 to 13.2), and one point of total about 2.2 points. Break-even at -110 needs 52.38%, so a bet must be about 0.75 to 0.8 points better than the fair spread, or about 1.1 points better than the fair total, just to break even.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of NBA closing-line accuracy (spread, total, moneyline) for 2018-19 onward was found. The recent-era numbers above come from a hobby GitHub project, a student capstone, a data site and one economist's blog.
- No measured comparison of Pinnacle or Circa closing accuracy against DraftKings or Fanatics closing accuracy on NBA games was found; the hold comparison is one week across ten leagues with NBA in preseason.
- No measured data on how fast NBA injury or lineup news is priced (seconds or minutes). Only qualitative statements exist.
- DraftKings and Fanatics opening times and limits for NBA sides and totals are not published; the Pinnacle figures come from a help page that now returns 404.
- The TeamRankings tables do not say which book's opening line is used and do not give results measured against the opening line for spreads.
- The season range and sample of Gandar et al. (1998, 2000) could not be read from the abstract pages.

## 2. What do academic and well-documented public machine-learning models achieve (winner accuracy, error against the spread, against-the-spread win rate, return on investment)?

### Takeaway
Honest, chronologically tested NBA models pick 64-68% of winners against 68-69% for the bookmaker, miss the margin by about 0.2 points more than the spread, and earn between roughly -5% and +3% on turnover, with the positive end needing low-margin (Pinnacle) odds and portfolio-style staking. Every result above about 72% winner accuracy or above 55% against the number that was checked here rests on leakage, confidence filtering, a single season or a few hundred games.

### Cited Findings

#### Careful, chronologically tested studies
- Hubáček, Šourek and Železný (International Journal of Forecasting 2019; numbers from the thesis reprint): box scores 2000-2014, expanding-window test on seasons 2006-2014, 9,093 games, Pinnacle closing odds for 2010-2014 and other books earlier. Winner accuracy without odds as an input: 67.47% (team-level network) and 67.62% (player-level convolutional network); with odds as an input: 68.83% and 68.80%; bookmaker 69%. Correlation of model and bookmaker probabilities: 0.87 without odds, 0.95 with. [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis, tables 19.2-19.3](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf); abstract at [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p783-796.html)
- Same study, returns (I read the table units as percent of amount staked; averages of 10 training runs): flat staking on every perceived value bet lost money in every configuration (-0.39% to -5.12%). Sharpe-ratio portfolio staking ranged from -1.92% to +1.74% depending on how strongly the model was decorrelated from the bookmaker. Adding a confidence filter lifted the best cases to +3.24% (4,228 games bet) and +3.39% (4,336 games), but a stricter filter flipped them to -5.41% and -5.06% (1,841 and 1,940 games). [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- The published abstract describes the same experiment as NBA seasons 2007-2014 with consistently positive cumulative profit for the combined method, while the thesis text says evaluated seasons 2006-2014. — [IDEAS abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p783-796.html)
- Manner (2016): team-strength regression, heteroscedastic and Kalman-filter state-space models, 2006-07 to 2013-14, second half of each season out of sample: MAE 9.36 to 9.38 against 9.17 for the opening spread; winners 68.1-68.4% against 69.2%. An equal-weight blend of model and spread scored MSE 137.93 against 137.49 for the spread alone and 69.3-69.4% winners. No betting profit is claimed. [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- Walsh and Joshi (Machine Learning with Applications, 2024): Basketball-Reference data 2014-15 to 2018-19; train 2014-16, validate 2016-17, test 2017-18, bet on 2018-19 only, against Westgate closing moneylines. Logistic regression, random forest, support vector machine and multilayer perceptron scored 65.34% to 66.55% on the 2017-18 test season; in the 2018-19 betting season the two chosen models scored 64.27% and 64.62%. — [Walsh and Joshi, arXiv 2303.06021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021)
- Same paper, betting: the models bet on 87.6% and 89.9% of games and won 38.8% and 38.5% of bets (mostly underdogs). With $100 flat bets from a $10,000 bankroll the calibration-selected model finished at $13,244.51 and the accuracy-selected model at $10,556.29; with one-eighth Kelly staking they finished at $13,692.86 and $2,409.66. The paper's headline "ROI" figures (+34.69% against -35.17% on average) are changes in the starting bankroll, not profit per dollar staked, and the authors list the single test season as the main limitation. — [Walsh and Joshi, full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06021)
- A hobby AutoML project (AutoGluon, about 23,000 games 2006-2026, Vegas lines used both as a feature and as the benchmark) reports no winning record and states that consistently beating the Vegas line remains a challenge. — [NBA-Betting/NBA_Betting](https://github.com/NBA-Betting/NBA_Betting)
- A self-reported public model (XGBoost with isotonic calibration, seven features, chronological 60/20/20 split) scored 68.1% winners on 1,358 held-out games, which it compares with about 68% for Vegas; picks it rated above 65% won about 76%; no against-the-spread record or return is reported. — [nbaanalytics.com, How it works](https://www.nbaanalytics.com/how-it-works.html)
- A simple rule (opening spread plus season win percentage, bet when the perceived edge is 2%+) run chronologically over 2011-2021 (13,903 games, 4,615 bets) went 2,309-2,212-94, a 51.07% win rate against a 52.38% break-even, and took a $1,000 bankroll to $49 with capped quarter-Kelly staking. Anonymous low-quality site, but an honest negative result. — [Backtest page](https://samesolutions-equipment-service.pages.dev/bets/learn/backtest-results.html)

#### Results that should be discounted, and why
- Same-game leakage: a PLOS ONE (2024) XGBoost study on 3,710 games from 2020-21 to 2022-23 reports 93.3% accuracy and AUC 0.982, but its inputs are box-score differences from the game being predicted (72.0% using only first-half stats, 79.8% after three quarters), scored with ten-fold cross-validation and with no comparison to betting odds. — [Ouyang et al. 2024, PLOS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11265715/)
- Confidence filtering presented as accuracy: the 84.35% figure often quoted from Hubáček et al. is the hit rate on the 1,940 most confident games out of 9,093; on all games the same model scored 67.5%, and that filtered subset lost about 5% in the betting test. — [Hubáček thesis, table 19.3](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- Tiny samples and random cross-validation: an often-cited 74% neural-network accuracy came from 620 games, and a 67% naive Bayes result came from ten-fold cross-validation inside the single 2009-10 season. [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis, related work](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- A 2024 systematic review lists NBA accuracies ranging from 60.8% (k-nearest neighbours) through 65.2% (random forest), 69.7% (logistic regression), 71.5% (graph network plus random forest) and about 76% (clustering plus neural network) up to 88-94% and 91.8% in individual papers, and reports no basketball betting profit figure for any of them. — [Galekwa et al., arXiv 2410.21484](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21484v1)
- Single-season totals claim: student models trained on 2021-22 and bet on every 2022-23 over/under reported 56.3% to 56.6% winners and about $12,000 profit, although their squared error (324.5 to 336.7) was only about level with the Vegas total (332.1); the authors themselves say the models should not be used for betting without more back-testing. — [Carnegie Mellon capstone 2024](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2024/460files/team6.pdf)
- Partial-season claim: an Elo-based spread strategy was reported at +3.46% ROI for the part of 2019-20 played at the time of writing. [snippet] — [Medium, Elo Against the Spread](https://medium.com/sports-analytics/elo-against-the-spread-b70a6cc2210a)
- A popular open-source project (XGBoost and neural network, team data from 2007-08) merges sportsbook odds into its training data and does not state whether its split is chronological; its page currently states no accuracy figure. — [kyleskom/NBA-Machine-Learning-Sports-Betting](https://github.com/kyleskom/NBA-Machine-Learning-Sports-Betting)

### Inferences
- Walsh and Joshi's flat-bet result is about a 3% return on turnover for the better model and about 0.5% for the other (roughly 1,000 to 1,080 bets of $100 for profits of $3,245 and $556; my estimate, since the paper does not state the number of bets), from one season at one Las Vegas book's closing prices. The gap between the two models is well inside one season's noise.
- Across the careful studies the winner-accuracy gap between model families is about one percentage point, smaller than the season-to-season swing of the bookmaker's own accuracy (plus or minus 2.5 points). Winner accuracy cannot separate a good model from a mediocre one in one or two seasons.
- A realistic expectation for a free-data model evaluated honestly: 65-68% winners, spread MAE 0.2 to 0.5 points worse than the closing line, an against-the-spread record between 49% and 52% when betting every game at -110, and a return between about -5% and 0% unless bets are selective and early.
- The +1% to +3% returns in Hubáček et al. were earned at Pinnacle prices (the thesis shows a visible drop in bookmaker margin once Pinnacle becomes the only odds source in 2010-2014) with mean-variance staking over about 600 betting rounds. At DraftKings or Fanatics margins of about 4.9-5.1% the same edge would be at or below zero.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed NBA study from 2018-19 onward was found that reports a walk-forward against-the-spread win rate or closing-line value over several seasons. The strongest multi-season betting test found (9,093 games) ends in 2014.
- No careful published comparison of gradient-boosted trees or Bayesian hierarchical models against the closing line with betting returns was found; the gradient-boosting results located are either leaked (same-game features) or self-reported.
- The Medium Elo article and several review-listed papers could not be opened to check sample sizes and validation methods.
- FiveThirtyEight's published Elo/RAPTOR track record against the closing line was not found in scored form.

## 3. Which feature sets and model families win in careful comparisons, and what are the common mistakes?

### Takeaway
In careful comparisons no model family wins by a meaningful amount: simple team ratings with home-court and rest terms sit within 1-2 points of accuracy of neural networks, and the market line beats all of them and is not improved by blending. What separates credible from non-credible work is the testing: leakage of same-game or post-decision information, random splits, tuning or model selection on the season that is then reported, bankroll-based "ROI", and ignoring a 4.5-5% margin account for essentially all the eye-catching results.

### Cited Findings
- Ratings versus the market: a team-strength regression with home advantage (estimated at about 2.7 points) and a back-to-back penalty (about -1.8 points) reached 68.2% winners and MAE 9.36; adding team-specific variance or a Kalman-filter time-varying strength did not help (68.1%, 68.4%), and within-season changes in team strength were too small to estimate reliably. 2006-07 to 2013-14. [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- Blending with the market: model and spread forecasts were more than 0.9 correlated; a 50/50 blend did not beat the spread on squared error in the regular season (137.93 against 137.49) and gained 0.1 to 0.2 points of winner accuracy; in the playoffs the best blend edged the spread (MSE 147.69 against 148.96). [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- Deep learning versus simpler inputs: a convolutional network fed every player's box-score line beat a plain network on team totals by 0.15 points of accuracy without odds (67.62% against 67.47%) and was no better once odds were added (68.80% against 68.83%). [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis, table 19.2](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- Market line as a feature: adding bookmaker odds raised accuracy by about 1.3 points but raised the correlation with the bookmaker from 0.87 to 0.95; profit depended on being different from the bookmaker where it mattered, and whether the odds feature helped profit was inconclusive. Forcing full decorrelation collapsed accuracy to about 61% and lost money. [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- Model family: on one test season logistic regression, random forest, support vector machine and multilayer perceptron were within 1.2 points of each other (65.34% to 66.55%); selecting by calibration error instead of accuracy changed the betting outcome far more than the choice of family did. 2014-15 to 2018-19. — [Walsh and Joshi 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06021)
- Staking interacts with calibration: one-eighth Kelly turned the less well calibrated model's season into a 76% loss of bankroll while flat stakes left it slightly positive. 2018-19, one season. — [Walsh and Joshi 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06021)
- No family dominates across the literature: logistic regression, naive Bayes and k-nearest neighbours top individual comparisons about as often as tree ensembles do. — [Galekwa et al. systematic review, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21484v1)
- In a seven-feature gradient-boosted model the Elo rating gap was the most important input, ahead of rolling point differentials (5, 10 and 20 games), rest gap and back-to-back flags. Self-reported. — [nbaanalytics.com](https://www.nbaanalytics.com/how-it-works.html)
- A professional's view of the same question (NFL, 2018): a model does not need to beat the market overall, only to contain information the market lacks; his best blend was about 45% model and 55% market, and he bets more as the two disagree more. — [RotoWire Q&A with Rufus Peabody](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article.php?id=40172)
- Mistake, mixed odds sources: the Hubáček test used several bookmakers before 2010 and Pinnacle after, and the authors note this produces artificially higher profit in the later seasons because Pinnacle's margin is smaller. [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis, section 19.2](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)
- Mistake, same-game features and random folds: see the 93.3% PLOS ONE result above. — [Ouyang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11265715/)
- Mistake, model choice made on the reported season and bankroll-based ROI: see Walsh and Joshi above, where the 2017-18 test season picked the model and one further season carried the whole conclusion. — [Walsh and Joshi 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06021)
- Mistake, software defaults: the first live version of Voulgaris's simulator assumed 88% free-throw shooting instead of about 75%, which turned every projection into an over. [pre-2018] — [ESPN The Magazine profile, 2013](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/dollars/post/_/id/2935/meet-the-worlds-top-nba-gambler)

### Inferences
- A sensible build order follows from this: (1) a ratings baseline (Elo or regression strength, home court, rest and back-to-back), (2) availability-adjusted team strength from player-level inputs, since injuries move lines by up to 5.5 points and are where the market says the information is, (3) gradient-boosted or regularised linear models on top. Model family is a second-order choice; availability and timing are first-order.
- Test design that the evidence supports: walk-forward by date (train on everything before, predict the next day or week); features built only from games already completed; hyperparameters and feature sets frozen before the reporting seasons; report margin MAE and RMSE, total MAE and RMSE, and log loss, Brier score and a calibration table for win probability, each beside the closing line's score on the same games.
- Keep two separate uses of the market line apart. As a benchmark, compare with the closing line. As an input, use only the line that was available at the moment the model would have bet, and score the bet against the later close; a model fed the closing line and bet at the closing line can only rediscover the margin.
- Returns must be per dollar staked at the actual price (-110, or the book's real moneyline), with pushes handled and with the number of bets stated. Any pregame winner accuracy above about 70% over a full season should be treated as a leakage alarm.
- Because good models are more than 0.9 correlated with the spread, most "value bets" are small disagreements inside the noise. Bet selection should require a disagreement larger than the break-even gap (about 0.8 points on spreads, 1.1 on totals) plus the model's own uncertainty.

### Gaps
- No careful head-to-head of "ratings plus market line" against deep learning on post-2018 NBA data with betting returns was found; the comparison above is pieced together from separate studies that end in 2014 or 2019.
- The systematic review's sections on overfitting and validation could not be read in full (page truncated), so its own statement of common errors is not quoted.
- No public study was found that measures how much player-availability features add over team-level ratings against the closing line.

## 4. Which NBA market inefficiencies or biases have been documented, and did they survive into recent seasons? (totals, back-to-backs, home underdogs, late season, public-team bias, early season)

### Takeaway
Almost every NBA bias in the academic record was measured before 2013, and the ones that were re-tested weakened or disappeared: unders on the highest totals (no longer a rejectable fair bet in 2012-19) and shading on popular teams (absent in 2012-17). In samples of 20,000+ games every split by opening total, spread size or line movement sits between about 48% and 52%. What is left from recent seasons are media "systems" of a few hundred games each, mostly built on DraftKings money percentages, none tested out of sample.

### Cited Findings

#### Totals
- 1995-96 to 2001-02: the totals market was efficient overall, except that unders on the highest totals beat a fair bet. [pre-2018] — [Paul, Weinbach and Wilson 2004, record](https://econbiz.de/Record/efficient-markets-fair-bets-and-profitability-in-nba-totals-1995-96-to-2001-02-paul-rodney/10007646715), findings as summarised in [Moore 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v82y2021icp26-29.html)
- Re-test on 2012-13 to 2018-19: the market is more efficient than in the earlier period; a fair bet is no longer rejected for the higher totals, and win rates above 52.4% appear only further out in the upper tail. The number of games is not on the abstract page. — [Moore 2021, Quarterly Review of Economics and Finance](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/quaeco/v82y2021icp26-29.html)
- Bettors lean over: overs take a much larger share of bets than unders and the share rises with each point of the total. [pre-2018] [snippet] — [Paul and Weinbach 2008, International Journal of Sport Finance](https://fitpublishing.com/node/944)
- By level of opening total, overs against the closing total (about 23,000 games through 2025-26): 51.9% at 185.5 or lower (n = 908), 51.7% at 186-190.5 (n = 1,300), 50.3% at 191-195.5 (n = 1,867), 49.3% at 196-200.5 (n = 2,042), 49.6% at 201-205.5 (n = 2,121), 50.5% at 206 or higher (n = 14,941). — [TeamRankings](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/over-under/over/)
- DraftKings money splits since January 2023: when most of the money was on the under, the under went 545-452 (54.6%, ROI +4.8%, n = 997); when most money was on the under after the total had risen from the open, 220-166 (57.0%, n = 386). The same article also lists a winning over system (77%+ of money on the over after the total rose: 350-274, 56.1%, n = 624). Media-compiled, in-sample. — [VSiN](https://vsin.com/nba/nba-betting-splits-strategies-for-the-2026-2027-season/)
- A one-season trend report had unders at 212-168-1 (55.8%) when the total fell from open to close. [snippet] — [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/nba-betting-trends-stats-notes-for-after-the-all-star-break). The long-run table shows the opposite sign: after the total moved down, overs hit 51.1% against the close (n = 10,943). — [BetIQ / TeamRankings](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/nba/betting-trends/over-under-results-by-line-movement-and-opening-line/)

#### Early season
- Totals lines were biased early in each season while side lines were not; movement from open to close went the right way without fully removing the bias. [pre-2018] [snippet; abstract page had no text] — [Baryla, Borghesi, Dare and Dennis 2007, Finance Research Letters](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612307000177)
- 2009 to 2012 (four seasons): 58.2% of first-week games stayed under the total, an average return of 11.1% per game for blind unders; after week 1 the market was efficient, with totals lines and scoring rising together over the first 17 weeks. The number of first-week games is not in the abstract. [pre-2018] — [Girdner, Davis, Fodor and Kirch 2013, Journal of Prediction Markets](https://www.ubplj.org/index.php/jpm/article/view/601)

#### Back-to-backs, rest and travel
- Over a 19-year sample, home teams on the second night of a back-to-back covered poorly when the visitor had one or two days of rest, more so after travelling one or two time zones east. No effect size is given on the abstract page. [pre-2018] — [Ashman, Bowman and Lambrinos 2010, Journal of Sports Economics](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:11:y:2010:i:6:p:602-613)
- In a ratings model a back-to-back costs about 1.8 points of margin and home court is worth about 2.7 (2006-07 to 2013-14). This is an effect on the score, not evidence of mispricing. [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- Since 2005, betting against any team on a back-to-back went 4,137-4,087-148 (50.3%) outside one narrow spot; only in the last games before the All-Star break was it 79-55-3 (58.9%). [snippet] — [Action Network / Bet Labs](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/nba-betting-system-pick-pre-all-star-break-trend)
- Recent small samples: home teams on 3+ days of rest against opponents on one day of rest went 75-47-2 against the spread over four seasons; road teams on one day of rest against home teams on a back-to-back went 47-39 since the start of the previous season. [snippet] — [VSiN daily trends](https://vsin.com/nba/steve-makinens-nba-betting-trends-and-best-bets-for-tuesday-february-24/)

#### Home underdogs and favourites
- 1995-96 to 2001-02: bettors over-bet favourites; big underdogs beat a fair bet, big home underdogs beat break-even, and betting against winning streaks was profitable. [pre-2018] — [Paul and Weinbach 2005, Journal of Sports Economics](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:6:y:2005:i:4:p:390-400)
- 2008 to 2018: home favourites covered 49.4% and home underdogs 48.4%. [snippet; sample not seen] — [Gambling.com NBA spread guide](https://www.gambling.com/us/sportsbooks/strategy/nba-spread-betting-guide-how-to-find-value-against-the-line-1533800)
- By size of spread, games where the line did not move: favourites of 1-2.5 covered 47.7% (n = 733), 3-6.5 covered 51.7% (n = 1,691), 7-9.5 covered 51.0% (n = 796), 10-13.5 covered 49.6% (n = 527), 14+ covered 52.2% (n = 117). No big-underdog effect is visible. — [TeamRankings](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/ats/movement/)
- DraftKings splits: majority of money on a home underdog went 165-131 (55.7%, n = 296, four seasons); 70%+ of tickets on the home side went 113-155 (42.2%, n = 268, since January 2023). Media-compiled, in-sample. — [VSiN](https://vsin.com/nba/nba-betting-splits-strategies-for-the-2026-2027-season/)

#### Public-team (sentiment) bias
- 1981 to 2012, more than 32,000 games: bookmakers shaded spreads on popular home teams (popularity measured by arena fill and All-Star votes), and the shaded prices did not give bettors on the other side higher returns. [pre-2018] — [Feddersen, Humphreys and Soebbing 2018, Journal of Sports Economics](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jospec:v:19:y:2018:i:4:p:455-472)
- 2012-13 to 2016-17: the same authors find popularity-driven bias in NFL betting but not in NBA betting. [pre-2018] — [Feddersen, Humphreys and Soebbing 2020, Applied Economics](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:applec:v:52:y:2020:i:53:p:5797-5806)
- 2025-26 at DraftKings: the side with most tickets covered 48.9% (625-652). — [VSiN](https://vsin.com/nba/nba-betting-splits-strategies-for-the-2026-2027-season/)

#### Late season and tanking
- Betting markets behave as if teams tank: spreads on affected late-season games shift systematically (by 1 to 4 points per a search summary of the working paper, 2003-04 to 2008-09), while the evidence that teams actually lose on purpose is mixed. [pre-2018] — [Soebbing and Humphreys, University of Alberta working paper 2010-13](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ris:albaec:2010_013)
- An oddsmaker says current lines are shaded somewhat further against tanking teams. [snippet] — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/114641/how-bookmakers-are-adjusting-to-nba-tankathon)
- In 2025-26 blowout rates rose sharply from February onward, especially in games involving tanking teams; the writer expects that to fade in 2026-27 under the new lottery rules. — [ESPN, new lottery and anti-tanking rules](https://www.espn.com/story/_/id/50079138)
- Strong teams such as San Antonio measurably weakened late in regular seasons when resting players before the playoffs (2006-14). [pre-2018] — [Manner 2016](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Survival scorecard. High-total unders: did not survive (2012-19 re-test). Popular-team shading: did not survive (2012-17 re-test). Week-1 unders, rest and travel spots, big home underdogs: never re-tested on post-2018 data that I could find, and the 20,000-game tables show nothing at the aggregate level. Generic back-to-back fades: priced in (50.3%). Late-season tanking: priced by the market and about to change regime.
- Fading the public is not a strategy either. If ticket-majority sides cover 48.9%, the other side covers 51.1%, still below the 52.4% break-even.
- The recent "systems" are a multiple-comparisons exercise. With n near 400 the standard error of a win rate is 2.5 points, so one or two results at 55-57% are expected from a list of 14 with no real effect, and the VSiN list contains winning systems that point in opposite directions on the same situation.
- Useful reading for the model: treat rest days, back-to-back flags, time zones crossed, week of season, opening-total level and tanking incentives as candidate features for predicting the score. The evidence says they move scores (about 1.8 points for a back-to-back) and that the market already knows it, so expect them to improve accuracy toward the line, not past it.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed re-test on 2018-19+ data was found for week-1 unders, rest and travel spots, or home underdogs.
- The Sport Journal article (totals and sides 2000-01 to 2007-08) and the full text of Soebbing and Humphreys could not be opened; a secondary summary mentioning profitable home underdogs of 10+ points in 2001-2008 and a 54.6% rested-versus-unrested result could not be traced to a verifiable paper and are left out of the findings.
- No league-wide, multi-season against-the-spread tables by rest situation for 2018-26 were retrieved; the VSiN and Action Network numbers are short-window trend notes.

## 5. How do professional sports bettors and syndicates actually build and use NBA models?

### Takeaway
There are two trades. Originators build their own price bottom-up (the best-documented NBA example simulates every possession from player-level ratings, with separate models for lineups and minutes), bet early while limits are low and at books that take sharp action, and judge themselves by whether the line then moves their way. Top-down bettors build no model: they treat the de-vigged price of the sharp books as the truth and pick off slower retail books. Both live on roughly 1-6% of turnover, both treat injury and lineup information as a core asset, and both are limited quickly at US retail books, where beating the close is the main trigger.

### Cited Findings

#### Originating (bottom-up)
- Voulgaris's "Ewing" model simulates each game possession by possession, tens of thousands of times per matchup; gives every player separate offensive and defensive values that change with the matchup; includes position-specific ageing curves; and is fed by two helper models, one projecting lineups and minutes and one tracking rosters, trades and draft picks. It outputs a projected score and a confidence number, and he bets above a confidence threshold. [2013] — [ESPN The Magazine profile](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/dollars/post/_/id/2935/meet-the-worlds-top-nba-gambler)
- Building it took about two years with a quantitative partner; the first live season (2008) was only slightly better than break-even, and results improved after a further component was added in 2009. Volume went from about 350 bets a season as a subjective bettor to well over 1,000. [2013] — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/dollars/post/_/id/2935/meet-the-worlds-top-nba-gambler)
- His earlier edges were structural rather than statistical: from 2002, books split full-game totals about evenly between halves and ignored higher late-game scoring, and he also tracked specific coaches' tendencies. When books changed their halftime method he lost a third of his bankroll in the last month of 2003-04. [pre-2018] — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/dollars/post/_/id/2935/meet-the-worlds-top-nba-gambler)
- He describes every bet as teaching the bookmaker, and his return fell from above 6% (2010-11) to 5.14% (2011-12) and lower in 2012-13. [pre-2018] — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/dollars/post/_/id/2935/meet-the-worlds-top-nba-gambler)
- Bottom-up betting ("originating") is defined as building predictive models that price events better than the sportsbook; top-down bettors follow market signals instead. [snippet] — [BetSmart newsletter](https://betsmart.beehiiv.com/p/bottom-up-betting)
- A football originator's working rules: model the process rather than the outcomes, accept that the market handles injuries and soft information better, blend model and market (about 45/55 for him), stake more as the disagreement grows, and expect a maximum bet to move the line. [2018, NFL] — [RotoWire Q&A with Rufus Peabody](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article.php?id=40172)
- Per a secondary summary of Miller and Davidow's The Logic of Sports Betting: prices are discovered at one main market-making book per sport and copied elsewhere, and bettors should aim at weak markets (few market-makers, low limits, thin information) rather than strong ones. [secondary summary, not checked against the book] — [Medium review](https://medium.com/@mosperityholdingsllc/the-logic-of-sports-betting-by-ed-miller-and-matthew-davidow-a-comprehensive-review-and-summary-ac7efc5f2ea)

#### Top-down (market-based)
- Top-down betting is steam chasing: it works only because recreational books copy market-makers with a lag and limit their customers; if every book made its own market, steam chasing and arbitrage would disappear. — [Jack Miller, Establish The Run, 2024](https://establishtherun.com/?p=812308)
- One well-known operation runs automated software that grades bets and flags line moves, injuries and news, watches sharp groups and an Asian book for NBA moves, does not watch games, and bets through hundreds of accounts with purpose-built betting robots. Its injury-sorting software took seven or eight years to refine. [2019] — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor); [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2019/06/05/gambling/sports-betting-bettors-sharps-kicked-out-spanky-william-hill-new-jersey)
- Commercial tools package the sharp consensus: Unabated publishes a vig-free line blended from sharp market-making books. [snippet] — [API review](https://sportsapis.dev/apis/unabated)

#### Speed on injury and lineup news
- The federal case shows what advance availability news is worth: one tip about a player leaving early was bought for $100,000 and about $259,000 was bet on that player's unders; a tip that Portland would sit its top scorers sold for $5,000 and drew more than $100,000 in bets against Portland before four top scorers were ruled out shortly before tip-off; another tip led to bets on Milwaukee before a Lakers star was announced out. Alleged conduct December 2022 to March 2024. — [NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/nba/news/what-to-know-about-terry-rozier-chauncey-billups-indictments-amid-sprawling-fbi-investigations)
- Books may hold a game off the board while a star is questionable. — [BettorEdge, October 2026](https://bettoredge.com/post/when-do-betting-lines-come-out)
- Oddsmakers' own player values (Jokic 5.5 points, most stars 2 to 4) show the size of the repricing a model must anticipate. — [theScore](https://www.thescore.com/nba/news/3531319/nba-playoff-injury-woes-how-much-are-stars-worth-to-their-teams)

#### Opening lines versus closing lines
- Low opening limits exist so sharp bettors move the number first; the best price is usually gone by the time limits rise, so professionals trade price against bet size. [2019] — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor)
- Books move lines aggressively in response to early bets. [2018] — [RotoWire, Peabody](https://www.rotowire.com/football/article.php?id=40172)
- A simple self-check: look at the price about 15 minutes after betting; if it moved toward your side the bet was probably good. — [Ben Porter](https://benporter.substack.com/p/where-do-odds-come-from)
- On NBA moneylines an economist's advice is to bet early; quotes a day out are where both the best and the worst prices are found. — [Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)

#### Closing-line value as the yardstick
- Beating the closing number is the measure this professional uses for himself. — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/whats-the-difference-between-you-and-a-sharp-step-inside-the-mind-of-a-professional-bettor)
- Sportsbooks use the same measure against customers: consistently beating the close is the most common way they identify winners, sometimes within hours of sign-up. — [Isaac Rose-Berman, How Gambling Works, 21 May 2025](https://howgamblingworks.substack.com/p/the-truth-about-limits)
- Caution for moneylines: on 3,670 NBA games about half of all quotes beat the close, yet three of the five positive-value deciles lost money because the closing prices themselves carry a 4.5% margin. — [Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)

#### Bankroll practice
- Margins are thin and volume is the business: two named professionals put their edge at about 1-2% of money wagered and say they need five- and six-figure bets or millions in turnover. [2019] — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2019/06/05/gambling/sports-betting-bettors-sharps-kicked-out-spanky-william-hill-new-jersey)
- Even the best-known NBA bettor describes chasing losses with bigger volume after his edge vanished in 2004 and taking half a season off. [pre-2018] — [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/blog/playbook/dollars/post/_/id/2935/meet-the-worlds-top-nba-gambler)
- Full Kelly staking is treated in the literature as close to certain ruin; fractional Kelly (one-eighth in this study) is the conservative norm, and Kelly staking with a poorly calibrated model lost 76% of bankroll in one season. — [Walsh and Joshi 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.06021)
- Spreading stakes with a mean-variance (Sharpe ratio) rule beat equal stakes in every configuration tested and roughly halved the variance of returns in simulation. [pre-2018] — [Hubáček thesis](https://dspace.cvut.cz/bitstream/handle/10467/114141/F3-D-2024-Hubacek-Ondrej-hubacek_thesis.pdf)

#### Account limiting at US books
- Signals believed to drive limits: closing-line value, what and when a customer bets, and other undisclosed variables; some customers are cut to single-digit dollars on some markets. — [Establish The Run](https://establishtherun.com/?p=812308)
- Speed: one professional was cut off by most New Jersey books within days, by DraftKings after one strong weekend; another bettor says most sharp accounts are limited after about four bets. Rufus Peabody's summary: "If you beat the line you get banned." [2019] — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2019/06/05/gambling/sports-betting-bettors-sharps-kicked-out-spanky-william-hill-new-jersey)
- Size of limits once restricted: typically a couple of hundred dollars on major markets and about $50 on props; the author's own Fanatics account was cut to $50 on major markets and $15 on props. FanDuel is described as offering the highest limits and as the only operator showing the maximum stake before a bet is submitted. Wyoming's regulator reports fewer than 1% of bettors limited. — [Rose-Berman, 2025](https://howgamblingworks.substack.com/p/the-truth-about-limits)
- Operators' own statements to the Massachusetts regulator (September 2024): BetMGM limits about 1% of patrons; Fanatics said more than 90% of its winning customers were never limited and nearly half of its limited customers were net losers while restricted; FanDuel said 0.043% of 2023 wagers were placed at the customer's maximum; DraftKings said limits target specific behaviour. — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/massachusetts-limiting-sports-betting-debate-sharps-roundtable-september-2024)
- Since 1 June 2026 Massachusetts requires a specific explanation within 48 hours. Early notices: DraftKings cited risk management after activity suggesting the targeting of perceived market inefficiencies; most Fanatics notices cited behaviour matching a network of restricted users signalling possible arbitrage. ### Inferences
- The builder is an originator by construction but lacks the two things professionals lean on: speed on news and access to books that do not limit. The realistic role of the model is narrower: form a price before the market has finished forming (overnight openers, the windows after the 5 p.m. day-before and 11 a.m.-1 p.m. game-day injury reports) and flag games where DraftKings or Fanatics sits away from both the model and the sharp consensus.
- Low opening limits do not bind a small bettor, so being early is feasible. The catch is that a retail opener is usually a copy of a number sharp bettors have already shaped, so the remaining window is short and mostly news-driven.
- Log for every bet or paper bet: timestamp, book, line and price taken, the same book's closing line and price, and a sharp or multi-book consensus close. That one table produces closing-line value in points and in probability, which is what both professionals and the books use.
- Plan for capacity, not just edge. If the model does beat the close at DraftKings or Fanatics, the documented response is a stake limit in the tens of dollars on main markets, arriving within days to weeks. Expected profit should be modelled as edge times a small, shrinking stake.
- Staking: flat or small fractional-Kelly stakes only after calibration has been checked out of sample; the published disasters come from Kelly applied to uncalibrated probabilities.
