# Probabilities and pricing: turning NBA point predictions into betting probabilities and fair prices

Scope: NBA only, US books, American odds. Written 19 November 2026 (2026-27 season under way). Evidence tags used throughout: **[P]** peer-reviewed / primary publication; **[D]** large public data table; **[H]** self-reported hobby/GitHub analysis (unaudited, treat as indicative); **[S]** seen only as a search-result snippet, the page itself could not be opened; **[OLD]** based on pre-2018-19 data, may be out of date. "My calc" means computed by this researcher from the cited tables; those numbers appear only under *Inferences* and should be re-derived on the project's own data.

## 1. The distribution of the NBA final margin around the closing spread, and of the final total around the closing total (SD by season in the 2020s, shape, and which distribution to use)

### Takeaway
The classic "SD = 12 points" for margin-vs-spread is out of date: measured closing-spread error in 2022-23 to 2024-25 is MAE 10.29 / RMSE 13.24 (MAE by season 9.78, 10.53, 10.56), up from about 9.1 MAE (about 11.4 SD) in 2006-2016; closing totals miss by MAE about 14.2-14.5, i.e. SD about 18-19. Every implementation found uses a normal (or a logistic for win probability), but the measured residuals are mildly fat-tailed and more peaked at the centre than a normal, and the margin has a hole at zero, so a single normal sigma cannot simultaneously price near-the-line bets, far alternate lines and moneylines.

**Shape and distributional form**
- [H] A normal predictive distribution for a margin model produced realised coverage of 49.0 / 78.1 / 93.0 % for nominal 50 / 80 / 95 % intervals ("slightly narrow, mostly in the tails"); for totals 52.0 / 81.5 / 95.6 % (slightly wide). — [roni-altshuler/nba_predictor](https://github.com/roni-altshuler/nba_predictor)
- [D] Density at the centre: whole-number closing spreads pushed 2.5-4.5 % of the time depending on the number, and whole-number totals about 1-5 % (full tables in section 2), 2003-04 to November 2026. — [TeamRankings spread records by closing spread](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/nba/betting-trends/by-closing-line/spread-records/); [TeamRankings over/under records by closing total](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/nba/betting-trends/by-closing-line/over-under-records/)
- [P][OLD] Stern (1991), NFL 1981/1983/1984: margin of victory over the spread is not significantly different from normal with mean zero and SD just under 14 (13.86), so P(favourite by p wins) = Phi(p/13.86). This is the template every NBA "Phi(spread/sigma)" formula copies. — [summary in arXiv 2212.08116](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08116)
- [P][OLD] Stern (1994): score difference modelled as Brownian motion with drift mu and variance sigma^2 over the game; "good fit" to 493 NBA games from 1991-92. — [Stern 1994 (JASA) PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/stern.pdf)
- [P][OLD] Gabel & Redner (2012), all 6,087 NBA games 2006/07-2009/10: scoring is well described by a continuous-time anti-persistent random walk (a score tends to be followed by an opponent score because possession changes); the model reproduces the distribution of final score differences. Theoretical basis for a near-Gaussian margin. — [arXiv 1109.2825](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.2825)
- Practitioner tooling: one public alternate-line calculator treats margins "as a normal distribution around your line, reweighted by how often each exact margin occurs", default sigma 13.5 (generic default, examples use football key numbers). — [BettorEdge alternate lines calculator](https://www.bettoredge.com/alternate-lines-calculator)
- Win probability is usually modelled with a logistic rather than a normal curve: the Wizard of Odds fits p = e^(-0.16 s)/(1+e^(-0.16 s)) to 2013-2017 data (see section 2). — [Wizard of Odds NBA](https://wizardofodds.com/games/sports-betting/nba/)

### Inferences
- **SD-equivalents by period (my calc).** Converting MAE to SD with the normal factor 1.2533, and alternatively with the measured pooled RMSE/MAE ratio 13.24/10.29 = 1.287: 2006-2016 about 11.4-11.7; 2015-21 about 12.4-12.7; 2018-19 to 2022-23 pooled about 12.9-13.2; 2022-23 about 12.3-12.6; 2023-24 about 13.2-13.6; 2024-25 about 13.2-13.6; "2020-2026" about 13.1-13.5. Planning value for margin-vs-closing-spread in the mid-2020s: **sigma about 13.2-13.5**, not 12. The 2026-27 blowout-heavy start suggests it is not falling.
- **Totals SD (my calc).** MAE 14.16-14.45 implies SD 17.7-18.1 under normality and about 18.2-18.6 if totals have the same RMSE/MAE ratio as margins; the one quoted "realised" figure is 18.7. Planning value: **sigma about 18-19** for final total vs closing total; the single 20.72 RMSE figure is unverified and should not be used until reproduced.
- **Tails (my calc).** MAE/RMSE = 0.777 for the 2022-25 spread residuals versus 0.798 for a normal, 0.764 for a logistic, 0.707 for a Laplace; a Student-t matches 0.777 at roughly 11-12 degrees of freedom. A t with 11 df and the same SD has about 8 % more density at the centre than the normal. Evidence is one hobby source, so treat "t with about 10-15 df" as a hypothesis to test on your own residuals, not a constant.
- **Centre of the distribution (my calc from TeamRankings, pooled 2003-04 to Nov 2026).** Pooled push rate on whole-number spreads is 3.64 % (517 / 14,203), i.e. about 3.6 % of probability per point at the line, equivalent to a normal with sigma about 11.0; pooled push rate on whole-number totals is 2.47 % (361 / 14,593), equivalent to sigma about 16.1. Both are tighter than the measured SDs (about 12-13.2 and about 18), i.e. "peaked centre plus fat tails". Practical consequence: near the main line each point of spread is worth about 3.0-3.6 points of cover probability and each point of total about 2.1-2.5 points; far alternate lines need the fatter tail.
- **No visible widening of the centre with the level of the total (my calc).** Push rates on whole-number totals are 2.51 % (totals below 200; n=5,532), 2.40 % (200-219; n=5,532) and 2.52 % (220+; n=3,529); standard errors are about 0.2-0.26 points, so a 5-10 % SD increase cannot be excluded, but there is no sign of a large one.
- **How SD has scaled with scoring (my calc).** Totals noise went from 17.1 (2007-12, mean total about 199) to about 18-18.7 (2018-23, totals in the 220s): close to proportional to the square root of the total (sqrt(225/199) = 1.06). Margin noise went from 11.6 to 13.2 (+14 %), more than scoring alone explains, consistent with higher three-point volume adding variance. A defensible rule if you want heteroskedasticity: sigma_total proportional to sqrt(predicted total); sigma_margin re-estimated on the last 2-3 seasons only.
- **The closing spread behaves like a median, not a mean (my calc from TeamRankings).** Favourites of 1-2.5 cover about 50 % but their average margin is 0.69 below the spread (n=5,783; SE about 0.17); 3-4.5: -0.77 (n=6,524); 5-6.5: -0.57; 7-8.5: -0.26; 9-14.5: about 0; 15+: +1.59 (n=636). Likely mechanism: no ties plus end-game fouling empties the 0-2 point region and stretches results away from zero, which moves the median more than the mean for small favourites. A model that predicts the *mean* margin should therefore expect to sit about 0.5-0.8 points toward the underdog relative to small closing spreads without having any real edge. The margin figures were transcribed by an automated page reader; verify before relying on them.
- **Joint (bivariate) view (my calc).** With equal team variances, sigma_T^2 = 2 s^2 (1+rho) and sigma_M^2 = 2 s^2 (1-rho), so rho = (sigma_T^2 - sigma_M^2)/(sigma_T^2 + sigma_M^2). Plugging sigma_T = 18.0-18.7 and sigma_M = 13.24 gives per-team residual SD about 11.2-11.5 and a between-team residual correlation of about +0.30 to +0.33 (2007-12 figures give 10.3 and +0.37). Under that model margin and total errors are uncorrelated (Cov = Var(home) - Var(away) = 0), which is why side and total can be priced from two separate marginals to first order.
- **Recommended form for the project.** (1) Estimate residual SDs from the model's own walk-forward residuals, by season, and compare with the market's 13.2 / 18-19; (2) use the normal for bets within about 5 points of the line, check a t (10-15 df) for far alternates; (3) do not derive moneyline probabilities from the same sigma — fit that map separately (section 2).

### Gaps
- No authoritative, published season-by-season table (2018-19 through 2025-26) of the SD of margin-minus-closing-spread and total-minus-closing-total was found; the 2020s numbers above come from unaudited GitHub projects that mostly report MAE, and nothing separate exists for 2025-26 or 2026-27 to date. This is cheap to compute directly once the project has closing lines.
- No rigorous 2020s test of whether residual SD depends on the size of the spread or of the total; the only direct evidence is an undated Boyd's Bets note (correlation 0.33 for totals).
- No source was found documenting that professionals use Student-t, kernel/empirical, Skellam or bivariate-Poisson models for NBA sides and totals; Unabated and Bet Labs explainers on this could not be located by search. Normal (plus empirical push-rate reweighting) is the only practice documented.
- Formal normality tests (skewness, kurtosis) of NBA spread/total residuals for 2018-19 onward were not found; overtime frequency and its effect on the right tail of totals was not sourced.
- Hal Stern's NBA-specific follow-up numbers (drift and variance in the 1994 paper) could not be read from the PDF in this session.

## 2. Converting a spread into a moneyline / win probability; alternate lines, the value of a half point, key numbers and push rates

### Takeaway
Empirically (29,718 games since 2003-04) NBA favourites of 3 / 5 / 7 / 10 / 13 points win about 57 / 66 / 72 / 80 / 90 % of the time; neither Phi(spread/12) nor the popular logistic with 0.16 per point fits that curve across the whole range, so the spread-to-moneyline map should be fitted empirically. Whole-number spreads push about 3.6 % of the time on average (about 2.7 % at 1-2, about 4.5 % at 5-7) and whole-number totals about 2.5 %, which makes a half point worth roughly 1.3-2.4 points of win probability versus the roughly 2.2 points that a 10-cent surcharge costs.

- Logistic form: for spreads beyond +/-15.5 the Wizard of Odds uses p = e^(-0.16 s)/(1+e^(-0.16 s)) with s the point spread (negative for the favourite); his "estimated probability" column for -1 to -15.5 follows the same curve. ### Inferences
- **Single-parameter fits to the TeamRankings table (my calc, 29,718 favourite games, 2003-04 to Nov 2026).** Maximum-likelihood probit P = Phi(spread/sigma): sigma = 11.41 (95 % CI 11.12-11.72). Logistic P = 1/(1+exp(-k*spread)): k = 0.1435 (95 % CI 0.1395-0.1475). The probit fits slightly better than the logistic (log-likelihood -17,527 vs -17,537), but both are rejected against the raw table (deviance about 82 and 103 on about 45 df). Using the residual sigma from section 1 (13.24) or the Wizard's k = 0.16 fits worse still (log-likelihood -17,580 and -17,569).
- **Where the simple curves go wrong (my calc).** Empirical favourite win % by bucket versus Phi(spread/11.41): spreads 1-2.5: 53.6 vs 56.3; 3-4.5: 60.5 vs 62.9; 5-6.5: 68.3 vs 69.3; 7-8.5: 74.6 vs 75.0; 9-10.5: 81.4 vs 80.2; 11-12.5: 85.6 vs 84.6; 13-14.5: 90.1 vs 88.4; 15+: 95.4 vs 92.3. The true curve is flatter than any normal/logistic near pick'em and steeper for big favourites; "implied sigma" (spread / Phi^-1(win %)) falls from about 20 at 1-2.5 to 14 at 3-4.5, 12 at 5-6.5 and about 10-11 beyond 9.
- **The Wizard's 0.16 is too steep in the middle (my calc).** His fitted curve exceeds his own raw 2013-2017 frequencies at almost every spread from 3 to 8.5 (table above) and exceeds the larger TeamRankings sample by 3-5 points at spreads 3-8; it matches only the 9+ range. If FiveThirtyEight's 28 Elo points per point also applied to the NBA, the implied logistic coefficient would be 28*ln(10)/400 = 0.161, the same curve, with the same weakness.
- **Smoothed empirical table (my calc, pooling each whole number with the next half point; 95 % CI in brackets; fair moneyline).** -1/-1.5: 51.7 % [49.7-53.6], -107 | -2/-2.5: 55.1 % [53.4-56.8], -123 | -3/-3.5: 58.7 % [57.1-60.4], -142 | -4/-4.5: 62.2 % [60.6-63.9], -165 | -5/-5.5: 65.9 % [64.2-67.5], -193 | -6/-6.5: 70.8 % [69.2-72.5], -243 | -7/-7.5: 72.5 % [70.8-74.3], -264 | -8/-8.5: 77.1 % [75.3-78.9], -336 | -9/-9.5: 80.5 % [78.7-82.3], -413 | -10/-10.5: 82.5 % [80.5-84.5], -471 | -11/-11.5: 85.0 % [82.9-87.2], -568 | -12/-12.5: 86.4 % [84.0-88.8], -635 | -13/-13.5: 89.6 % [87.3-92.0], -864 | -14/-14.5: 90.8 % [88.1-93.6], -990. Pooled over 23 seasons, so it under-weights the higher-variance 2020s (which should make favourites slightly less safe at a given spread).
- **Use the predicted mean, not the spread, in a normal formula (my calc).** Regressing win on the favourite's *average realised margin* at each spread gives Phi(mean margin / 10.97) with a near-perfect fit (deviance about 20 on 45 df; bucket errors under 1 point). Because the same games generate both the mean and the win rate, part of that fit is circular, but the bucket-level gap between spread and mean margin (section 1) is statistically clear. Working rule for a model that outputs an expected margin m: P(win) = Phi(m / s_w) with s_w about 11 as a prior, then refit s_w (or a 2-parameter logistic / isotonic map) on the model's own out-of-sample predictions; expect s_w to be nearer 11.5-12 on 2020s-only data. Do not reuse the residual SD (13.2+) for this.
- **Cover, push and over/under probabilities from a predictive distribution F of the integer outcome X (standard continuity-corrected formulas).** Half-point line L: P(over/cover) = 1 - F(L). Whole-number line L: P(win) = 1 - F(L + 0.5); P(push) = F(L + 0.5) - F(L - 0.5); P(lose) = F(L - 0.5). Fair two-way price ignores pushes: p* = P(win)/(P(win)+P(lose)); fair American = -100 p*/(1-p*) if p* >= 0.5, else +100 (1-p*)/p*. A smooth F under-predicts pushes at 5-7 and over-predicts them at 1-2, so multiply the smooth push mass by an empirical ratio (about 1.2 at 5-7, about 0.75 at 1-2 and, on this sample, at 4 and 8) or use an empirical margin-frequency reweighting as the BettorEdge tool does.
- **Value of a half point (my calc from TeamRankings push rates).** Moving across half a point next to integer n converts P(margin = n) between push and win/loss, worth about P(n)/2 in push-adjusted win probability: about 1.3-1.5 points next to 1, 2, 4 or 8 (push 2.5-2.9 %), about 1.8-1.9 next to 3 (3.7 %), about 2.2-2.4 next to 5, 6, 7 (4.4-4.5 %); average about 1.8 (push 3.64 %). In fair-price terms that is about 5-6 cents (2.5 % push), 7-8 cents (3.5 %), 9-10 cents (4.5 %) per half point from an even-money base. A flat 10-cent charge raises break-even by 2.17 points (52.38 % to 54.55 %), so buying NBA half points is negative-EV everywhere except roughly break-even around 5-7. For totals the half point is worth about 1.2 points (2.47 % push), i.e. about 5 cents.
- **Per-point slopes for quick edge maths.** Near the line: spread about 3.0 % per point (normal, sigma 13.24) to 3.6 % (empirical); total about 2.1-2.2 % per point (sigma 18-19) to 2.5 % (empirical). Moneyline: the empirical table rises about 1.7-1.8 points of win probability per half point between spreads of 1 and 8, flattening beyond 10.
- **Pricing an alternate ladder.** (1) De-vig the main line (section 3) to get the market's cover probability at the main number; (2) solve for the location shift that makes the chosen distribution reproduce it; (3) read off every other number with the whole-number push treatment above. Expect book alternates to carry more hold than the main line, so compare each rung with the fair price, not with the main-line vig.

### Gaps
- No measured dataset of DraftKings or Fanatics alternate-spread / alternate-total ladders (cents per half point by distance from the main line, hold per rung) was found; the "10 cents per half point" figure is from generic guides seen as snippets.

## 3. Removing the bookmaker's margin (de-vigging): which method, and is there favourite-longshot bias in NBA moneylines?

### Takeaway
For a two-way market Shin's method is mathematically the additive method; power, additive/Shin and odds-ratio all give the favourite more probability than multiplicative normalisation, agreeing to within 0.1 point on -110-type spread/total prices and diverging by about 0.4 points at -150, 1.2 at -300 and 2+ at -700. The general literature prefers power or Shin over multiplicative, but the most recent NBA-specific evidence (2022-23 to 2024-25) reports no favourite-longshot bias in NBA moneylines, and no head-to-head accuracy test of the methods on NBA data was found, so the choice should be settled by a log-loss test on the project's own archive and only matters for heavy favourites.

### Cited Findings
- Method definitions (r_i = 1/decimal odds, n outcomes): multiplicative p_i = r_i / sum(r); additive p_i = r_i - (sum(r) - 1)/n; power p_i = r_i^(1/k) with k chosen so the p_i sum to 1 (Buchdahl calls this the logarithmic method); odds-ratio (Cheung 2015) p_i = r_i / (OR + r_i - OR*r_i) with OR chosen so the p_i sum to 1; "margin weights proportional to odds" (Buchdahl) p_i = (n - M*O_i)/(n*O_i); Shin (1992, 1993) assumes a fraction z of insider money and solves for z; also balanced-book and Jensen-Shannon variants. — [R package "implied" vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)
- "If there are only two outcomes, the additive method and Shin's method are equivalent"; the basic (multiplicative) method "tend[s] to be the least accurate of the methods in this package"; the additive method can give negative probabilities with many outcomes (not an issue with two). — [R package "implied" vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/implied/vignettes/introduction.html)
- [P] Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram (2017), three large bookmaker datasets in three sports: the power method "universally outperforms the multiplicative method and outperforms or is comparable to the Shin method"; normalisation "does not account for favorite long-shot bias"; "the Shin and additive methods are equivalent for races with two competitors". Not NBA. — [American Journal of Sports Science 5(6):45-49](https://www.sciencepublishinggroup.com/article/10.11648/j.ajss.20170506.12)
- [S] Secondary summary of the same paper: 14,925 ATP matches, average overround 6.1 %; for one example favourite, normalisation gives 71.8 %, additive/Shin 72.8 %, power 73.3 %. — [Matchstat](https://matchstat.com/predictions-tips/three-tennis-stats-to-use-when-assessing-match-odds)
- [P] Štrumbelj (2014, International Journal of Forecasting 30(4):934-943) is reported to show that Shin-derived probabilities predict outcomes better than basic normalisation; a 2022 replication on soccer found mixed results (Shin unbiased in the English Premier League, both methods biased in La Liga). Primary paper not opened. — [Annals of Operations Research 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-022-04722-3)
- Buchdahl lists four methods (equal margin, odds ratio, logarithm, Shin) for stripping Pinnacle's margin and calls equal margin "crude and frankly inaccurate"; he notes de-vigged values depend on what is assumed about favourite-longshot bias. — [Buchdahl, Pinnacle Odds Dropper blog](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)
- **NBA moneylines, 2022-23 to 2024-25 (3,670 games; median closing quote across about ten licensed US books via The-Odds-API):** described as "an efficient market, with no favorite-longshot bias", with a bookmaker margin of about 4.5 %. Asserted from the author's data rather than shown in a table. — [Whelan, "The Truth about Closing Line Value" (Aug 2026)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)
- [OLD] NBA 2013-2017 (6,506 games with a favourite): blind moneyline bets on favourites returned -3.75 %, on underdogs -4.21 %; spread bets on favourites -4.84 %, on underdogs -4.07 %. — [Wizard of Odds NBA](https://wizardofodds.com/games/sports-betting/nba/)
- [P] Moskowitz & Vasudevan, 36,609 US college and professional basketball and football games with both spread and moneyline prices: the standard favourite-longshot pattern appears in moneyline bets but not in the matching spread bets; spread bets lose about the same in every moneyline decile and lose "a lot less" than moneylines for the three longest-odds deciles. Pooled with college games (where extreme prices are far more common), not NBA-only. — [Whelan lecture slides summarising the paper](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Teaching/BettingMarkets/13.PointSpreadsandAsianHandicap.pdf); [NBER conference version, as identified by search](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f144718.pdf)
- [P] A reverse anomaly in the pandemic period: betting every NBA underdog on the moneyline returned +16.7 %, with most of the inefficiency in underdogs priced +233 to +400; not seen in other sports and attributed to the absence of crowds. — [Qureshi & Zaman, arXiv 2109.07581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07581)
- [H] 2022-23 to 2024-25 (n=3,687): mean de-vigged consensus P(home win) 0.5588 versus observed home win rate 0.5541; mean de-vigged P(home cover) 0.5000; the consensus close is described as well calibrated in every probability bin (no per-bin numbers). — [nba-beat-the-close RESULTS.md](https://github.com/DeliciousPipe1326/nba-beat-the-close/blob/main/docs/experiments/other_markets/RESULTS.md)
- [S] US book margins: a one-week hold report (12-18 Oct 2026) lists DraftKings median hold 4.27 % across 123 markets versus Pinnacle 2.90 %; a promotional comparison site estimates DraftKings NBA spread vig at about 4.8 %. No Fanatics figure found. — [SharpAPI hold report](https://sharpapi.io/research/hold-report); [Odds Reference](https://oddsreference.pages.dev/compare/draftkings-vs-fanduel)

### Inferences
- **Two-way closed forms.** With r1, r2 the raw implied probabilities (American -A: A/(A+100); +A: 100/(A+100)) and S = r1 + r2: multiplicative p1 = r1/S; additive = Shin p1 = r1 - (S - 1)/2; power p1 = r1^k with r1^k + r2^k = 1 (one-dimensional root find); odds-ratio p1 = r1/(c + r1 - c*r1) with c solving the sum-to-one condition (equivalent to subtracting a constant from both log-odds, i.e. the "logit" method).

- **Reading of the evidence for NBA two-way markets.** Spreads and totals: the method is irrelevant (differences under 0.1 point); use multiplicative. Heavy favourites: the methods disagree by 1-2.5 points, and the evidence conflicts — cross-sport studies say correct for favourite-longshot bias (power, Shin/additive), while 2022-25 NBA closing moneylines reportedly show none (which makes multiplicative right and the others over-corrections toward the favourite), and 2013-17 NBA returns show only a 0.5-point favourite/underdog gap. Additive/Shin or odds-ratio sit between the extremes and are a reasonable default until tested.
- **Test to run.** On every archived game compute the closing favourite probability under each method and compare log-loss and a reliability table restricted to favourites beyond -300; roughly 350-450 games a season have a favourite of 8 or more points (about -320 or shorter; 29.5 % of games since 2003-04, my calc from TeamRankings), giving a per-season standard error near 2 points on their win rate, so about four seasons are needed to separate methods that differ by 1-2 points.
- **Hold bookkeeping.** -110/-110 is a 4.76 % overround and a 4.55 % theoretical hold; break-even win rate 52.38 %. Compare model probabilities with the de-vigged probability to decide *whether* there is an edge and with the quoted price to compute EV.

## 4. Calibration and scoring of probabilities: Brier, log-loss, reliability tables, Platt / temperature / isotonic, and how many games are needed

### Takeaway
The bar is the closing market: log-loss about 0.58-0.625 per season and Brier about 0.207 on home-win probabilities, against which decent public models are worse by about 0.02-0.035 log-loss / 0.007-0.015 Brier. With only about 1,230 games a season, a ten-bin reliability table has standard errors of about 4.5 points per bin, isotonic regression is under-powered (it wants more than about 1,000 calibration samples), and a two-parameter logistic (Platt) recalibration pooled over two to three seasons is the method the sample size supports.

### Cited Findings
- [H] Closing-market benchmark, home-win probability from de-vigged consensus: log-loss 0.6250 (2022-23), 0.5806 (2023-24), 0.5818 (2024-25), pooled 0.5957 (n=3,687); two rating/efficiency models scored 0.6303 and 0.6239 pooled (+0.0346 and +0.0282) and were +0.0155 and +0.0126 worse on Brier. — [nba-beat-the-close RESULTS.md](https://github.com/DeliciousPipe1326/nba-beat-the-close/blob/main/docs/experiments/other_markets/RESULTS.md)
- [H] 2018-19 to 2022-23 (5,431 games): market home-win log-loss 0.6115 versus a simulation model's 0.6320; the gap's resampling interval excludes zero. — [DaSilvaDub/outlier PR #294](https://github.com/DaSilvaDub/outlier/pull/294)
- [H] 14,600 priced games: Brier 0.2070 for the closing line, 0.2141 for a margin model, 0.2444 for a constant base rate; the market's advantage of 0.0071 has a 95 % CI of [0.00573, 0.00849]; the model closes 81 % of the gap between base rate and market. — [roni-altshuler/nba_predictor](https://github.com/roni-altshuler/nba_predictor)
- Brier score and log-loss are strictly proper but mix calibration with discrimination: "A lower Brier loss ... does not necessarily mean a better calibrated model"; judge calibration with reliability curves or the decomposition. — [scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)
- Platt / sigmoid scaling fits p = 1/(1+exp(A f + B)) by maximum likelihood and "is most effective for small sample sizes"; isotonic regression is more flexible but "more prone to overfitting, especially on small datasets" and matches or beats sigmoid only "when there is enough data (greater than ~ 1000 samples)"; isotonic introduces ties in the predictions. — [scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)
- Temperature scaling divides the logits by one learned parameter T (fitted by minimising log-loss on held-out data) and does not change which side is favoured; calibrators must be fitted on predictions for data the model was not trained on (cross-validated or held-out). — [scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)
- Reliability diagrams bin predictions, plotting mean predicted probability against observed frequency per bin, with a histogram of counts per bin. — [scikit-learn calibration guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)
- [P] NBA-specific: choosing among machine-learning models by calibration rather than accuracy produced average ROI of +34.69 % versus -35.17 % (best case +36.93 % vs +5.56 %) in a one-season betting simulation; the authors conclude calibration matters more than accuracy for betting. Single season, so the magnitudes are not reliable; the direction is the point. — [Walsh & Joshi, arXiv 2303.06021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06021)
- Bookmakers themselves are ranked by Brier and rank-probability scores, with Pinnacle described as the most accurate. — [Buchdahl, Pinnacle Odds Dropper blog](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)

### Inferences
- **Season-to-season swings in the benchmark exceed model-vs-market gaps.** Market log-loss moved from 0.625 to 0.581 between 2022-23 and 2023-24 (a parity effect), about 1.5 times the typical model-minus-market gap of 0.03. Always score the model and the market on the identical set of games and report the difference, never the raw score.
- **Reliability-table precision (my calc, binomial SE = sqrt(p(1-p)/n)).** One regular season (1,230 games) in ten equal-count bins gives 123 games per bin: SE 4.5 points at p = 0.5, 4.1 at 0.7, 3.2 at 0.85. For a 95 % half-width of +/-3 points a bin needs about 1,070 games at p = 0.5 (about 800 at 0.75); for +/-2 points about 2,400. So: five bins over about four to five seasons for +/-3 points; ten bins at +/-2 points needs about 20 seasons, i.e. is not attainable with era-consistent data. Mirror-image home/away rows are not extra observations.
- **Detecting a Brier-score difference (my calc from the cited CI).** The 14,600-game interval implies a per-game SD of the Brier difference of about 0.085. Games needed for a 2-SE result: gap 0.007 (typical model vs market) about 570; 0.005 about 1,160; 0.003 about 3,200; 0.002 about 7,200; 0.001 about 29,000. Showing that a model *loses* to the market takes half a season; showing a small genuine improvement takes many seasons.
- **Calibration recipe the sample size supports.** (1) Produce walk-forward out-of-sample margins/totals; (2) map margin to win probability with a one- or two-parameter curve (Phi(m/s) or logistic a + b*m), which is Platt scaling on the margin; (3) check for needed "temperature" by regressing outcomes on the logit of the predicted probability (slope below 1 means over-confidence); (4) reserve isotonic regression for pooled samples of several thousand games; (5) report log-loss, Brier, and a five-bin reliability table with binomial intervals, all as differences from the de-vigged close.
- **For spread and total bets the calibration target is P(cover).** Bin predicted cover probabilities (most will lie between 45 % and 55 %) and check realised cover rates; with predictions that concentrated, resolution is tiny and thousands of bets are needed (see section 6), so calibrate the residual SD (interval coverage at 50 / 80 / 95 %) rather than the binary outcome.

### Gaps
- No published per-bin reliability table for NBA closing moneylines or spreads from 2018-19 onward was found (only statements that the close is well calibrated).
- No NBA-specific study of how many games are needed for stable calibration estimates (ECE, calibration slope) was found; the figures above are binomial arithmetic.
- Details of the Walsh & Joshi study (seasons, models, number of bets) were not available from the abstract page.

## 5. Combining a model's number with the market's: blending, shrinkage, regression tests, Benter-style two-step models

### Takeaway
Every recent NBA test found puts essentially all the weight on the closing line: in 2022-23 to 2024-25 regressions the close's coefficient is about 1.0 and the model's about +0.05 (95 % CI roughly -0.07 to +0.17) on margin, and negative on moneyline log-odds; a 2018-19 to 2022-23 blend improved log-loss by 0.0005 with an interval spanning zero. The established professional template (Benter) is exactly this second-stage regression of outcomes on model and market, with bets sized from the combined estimate — and against an efficient close, a small blend weight turns a multi-point raw disagreement into a fraction of a point of real edge.

### Cited Findings
- [H] Margin regression, final margin on closing spread and model prediction, 2022-23 to 2024-25 (n=3,687): for a pace/efficiency model the close's coefficient is 0.973 and the model's +0.052 (95 % CI -0.066 to +0.170); for an Elo-based model 1.015 and -0.009 (-0.132 to +0.114). Moneyline logistic blend: market coefficient 1.047 / model -0.030, and 1.092 / -0.090. Spread picks versus the consensus covered 50.3 % and 49.4 %. Conclusion: neither model adds information to the close. — [nba-beat-the-close RESULTS.md](https://github.com/DeliciousPipe1326/nba-beat-the-close/blob/main/docs/experiments/other_markets/RESULTS.md)
- [H] 2018-19 to 2022-23 (5,431 games): blending a simulation model into the market with weights learned on prior seasons changed log-loss by +0.0005 with an interval spanning zero; the market beat the model in every season; stated conclusion "model weight 0 is the correct setting". — [DaSilvaDub/outlier PR #294](https://github.com/DaSilvaDub/outlier/pull/294)
- [H] A margin/total model trained on 2004-2026 without market features trails the close by 0.0071 Brier, 0.31 points of margin MAE and 0.76 points of total MAE. — [roni-altshuler/nba_predictor](https://github.com/roni-altshuler/nba_predictor)
- [S][H] Opening versus closing: a recency-weighted totals model went 278-200 (58.2 %) in 2023-24 when it disagreed with the *opening* total by 5+ points, but the same picks graded at the *closing* total won about 51.3 %, because the line moved toward the model. — [RJMurph10/gamblingnfl PR #95](https://github.com/RJMurph10/gamblingnfl/pull/95)
- A public forecaster's choice: Neil Paine's 2023-24 NBA forecast offers a "Composite Mode" that blends ratings-based win probabilities with FanDuel market probabilities, and a stats-only mode. — [Neil Paine, 2023-24 NBA forecast](https://neilpaine.substack.com/p/the-messengers-2023-24-nba-forecast)
- [P][OLD] Benter (1994): a fundamental multinomial-logit model's probabilities are used "as inputs to a second stage model which also incorporates market generated probabilities" (a logit combining the two); he recommends at least 500-1,000 races for development and testing, and bets a fraction (1/2 or 1/3) of Kelly. A secondary blog reports the public odds alone at R^2 0.1237, the fundamental model alone at 0.1016, and the combination at 0.1327 over 2,313 races (unverified against the original). — [Benter 1994 (PDF)](https://www.gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf); [summary](https://cydalytics.blogspot.com/2020/01/bill-benter-1994-computer-based-horse.html); [secondary blog with the R^2 figures](https://oddspapi.io/blog/?p=3174)
- [P][OLD] Hubáček, Šourek & Železný (2019), NBA seasons 2007-2014: instead of maximising accuracy they reduce the model's correlation with the bookmaker's implied probabilities, and combine this with a convolutional network on player statistics and portfolio-style staking; the combination produced positive cumulative profit where accuracy-only alternatives did not. A reader's analysis reports the decorrelation weight working best in the range 0.4-0.8. — [International Journal of Forecasting 35(2):783-796](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v35y2019i2p783-796.html); [blog analysis](https://joe-ferrara.github.io/2020/05/04/basketball.html)
- [S] Miller & Davidow: line movement after a bet "reflects something of a consensus of all the serious bettors in the same market" — i.e. the market's later number is information to be weighed against one's own. — [Legal Sports Report excerpt of *The Logic of Sports Betting*](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/33079/the-logic-of-sports-betting-market-agreement-and-resistance/)

### Inferences
- **The regression tests to run (walk-forward, out-of-sample predictions only).** Margin: (final margin - close) = a + c*(model - close) + error, test c > 0 with game-clustered or date-clustered errors; equivalently margin = a + b*close + c*model. Totals: same with totals. Moneyline: logit P(home win) = a + b*logit(p_market) + c*logit(p_model). The fitted c is the optimal shrink weight: blended number = close + c*(model - close).
- **Benter's second stage in two-outcome form.** His combined probability is usually written c_i proportional to f_i^alpha * pi_i^beta (f = model, pi = public); with two outcomes that is exactly the moneyline logistic regression above without an intercept (stated from the researcher's prior knowledge of the paper; the formula could not be machine-read in this session and his fitted alpha and beta were not retrieved).
- **What a small c does to "edges" (my calc).** At -110 a spread bet needs about 0.65-0.8 points of true advantage over the fair line (2.38 points of probability divided by 3.0-3.6 % per point); a total needs about 1.0-1.1 points. With c = 0.05 a raw model-minus-close disagreement must exceed about 13-16 points before the blended number clears that bar; at the top of the measured interval (c = 0.17) about 4-5 points; at c = 0.25 about 3 points; at c = 0.5 about 1.5 points. Unshrunk disagreements of 2-4 points are therefore not bets against a closing line.
- **Power of the test (my calc).** The quoted interval implies SE(c) of about 0.06 with 3,687 games; a true c of 0.10 would need roughly 5,000+ games (four seasons) to be distinguishable from zero at 2 SE. A one-season backtest cannot validate a blend weight.
- **Where a one-person model can plausibly add information.** The cited tests are against the *close*. The opening-versus-closing result above, and the fact that DraftKings and Fanatics post numbers many hours before tip, suggest testing c separately against the price actually available at bet time (openers, overnight, stale numbers after injury news); a model that carries no weight at the close can still carry weight earlier.
- **Decorrelation as a design principle.** A model built from the same public box-score information the market already prices will have c near 0 however accurate it is; Hubáček et al. optimise for disagreement that is right rather than for accuracy. Features the market may under-weight are worth more than incremental accuracy.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed NBA study using 2018-19 or later data that estimates optimal model/market blend weights was found; the recent evidence is from unaudited GitHub projects whose models are simple rating systems.
- Benter's fitted alpha and beta and his original R^2 table were not read from the primary PDF; the R^2 numbers quoted are from a secondary blog.
- The book-length treatment in Miller & Davidow of blending/originating versus following the market was not accessible; only a publisher excerpt snippet was seen. Classic regression-efficiency papers on the NBA spread market (e.g. 1990s Journal of Finance work) were not retrieved.
- No evidence was found on blend weights specifically against DraftKings or Fanatics opening lines.

## 6. Bet sizing and proof of edge: Kelly under uncertainty, correlated same-game bets, EV with pushes, closing-line value, sample size at -110

### Takeaway
Full Kelly at -110 is already small (5.5 % of bankroll at a true 55 %) and should be shrunk by roughly half or more once the uncertainty in the model's probability is counted; a workable approximation is to multiply the Kelly stake by s^2 / (s^2 + ((b+1)/b)^2 * var(p)). Proving a win-rate edge from results takes thousands of bets (about 1,450-2,250 for a true 55 %, about 3,800-5,900 for 54 %), whereas closing-line value stabilises in tens to hundreds of bets — but in 2022-25 NBA moneylines only bets that beat the close by about the full bookmaker margin (about 5 %) actually broke even.

### Cited Findings
- Kelly fraction for a bet at net odds b with win probability p: f = p - (1 - p)/b. — [Morse, notes on Baker & McHale](https://stmorse.github.io/journal/odds4.html)
- [P] Baker & McHale (2013, Decision Analysis 10(3):189-199): when the win probability is an estimate, the Kelly stake should be shrunk; shrinking improves out-of-sample performance in simulation and on tennis betting data; they give an approximate "back of envelope" shrinkage factor. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ordeca/v10y2013i3p189-199.html)
- A reproduction of their Figure 1 (b = 1, true p = 0.7): with SD of the probability estimate 0.20 the optimal multiple of Kelly is about 0.5; with SD 0.05 it is close to 1; the paper proves that some shrinkage is always optimal under its assumptions. — [Morse, notes on Baker & McHale](https://stmorse.github.io/journal/odds4.html)
- [P][OLD] Benter's reasons for fractional Kelly: overestimating one's advantage by more than a factor of two gives negative capital growth; withdrawals make effective wealth smaller than nominal; full Kelly drawdowns of more than 50 % are "a common occurrence"; he recommends 1/2 or 1/3 Kelly. — [summary of Benter 1994](https://cydalytics.blogspot.com/2020/01/bill-benter-1994-computer-based-horse.html)
- [P] Uhrín, Šourek, Hubáček & Železný (2021), horse racing, basketball and soccer data under one protocol: formal Kelly / portfolio strategies need additional risk-control in practice, and an adaptive fractional Kelly is "a very suitable choice across a wide range of settings". — [arXiv 2107.08827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08827)
- [P] Whitrow (2007, JRSS-C 56(5):607-623): optimal staking on many simultaneous bets is a numerical log-utility optimisation that generalises Kelly staking; examples use real bookmaker odds. A later preprint characterises his singles-only solutions as close to proportional to the stand-alone Kelly stakes [S]. — [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssc/v56y2007i5p607-623.html); [arXiv 2603.26620](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26620v1)
- Closing-line value (decimal odds): odds taken divided by fair (margin-free) closing odds; expected value = offered odds / true odds (2.10 / 2.00 = 5 %). — [Buchdahl, Pinnacle Odds Dropper blog](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)
- Buchdahl's evidence that CLV tracks profit (soccer, Pinnacle close): across nearly 20,000 bets realised profit on turnover of 3.4 % against a closing-line expectation of 4.0 %; in a 952-bet sample 756 saw the price shorten or hold, average shortening 3.94 %, and 65 bets would have sufficed to establish the effect statistically. Per-bet SD is about 1.00 for even-money profit but about 0.1 for CLV, so a 5 % edge needs "several thousand bets" to show in results but "perhaps as few as just 50" via CLV if the close is beaten consistently. He cautions that closing-odds accuracy varies by market and bookmaker. — [Buchdahl, Pinnacle Odds Dropper blog](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)
- **NBA counter-evidence, moneylines 2022-23 to 2024-25** (3,670 games; 34,944 quotes per timestamp from about ten US books; CLV measured against the median closing quote): positive-CLV quotes did better than negative-CLV ones, but three of the five positive-CLV deciles did not make a profit on average (the source sentence is garbled); the ninth decile averaged 5 % CLV for a 0.2 % profit and only the top decile was clearly profitable (+11.4 %). For quotes taken 24 hours before tip the top decile returned +16 % and the bottom decile -25 %; the overall average loss was about 4.5 %. "You gotta beat it enough to overcome the bookmaker's vig." — [Whelan, "The Truth about Closing Line Value" (Aug 2026)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595)
- [S] Miller & Davidow's rule of thumb: an average closing-line value "of at least half the hold" means "you're probably on the right track". — [Legal Sports Report excerpt of *The Logic of Sports Betting*](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/33079/the-logic-of-sports-betting-market-agreement-and-resistance/)
- [S] Scale of luck in results (golf betting analysis, not NBA): below about 5,000 bets randomness still plays a meaningful role, and even at 10,000 bets chance deviations of 2-3 % are possible. — [Data Golf](https://datagolf.com/how-sharp-are-bookmakers)
- -110 on both sides is decimal 1.909 and a 4.5 % margin; -110 to -120 raises the break-even win rate from 52.4 % to 54.5 %. — [Whelan lecture slides](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Teaching/BettingMarkets/13.PointSpreadsandAsianHandicap.pdf); [Action Network: buying points](https://www.actionnetwork.com/education/buying-points)

### Inferences
- **EV and Kelly with pushes (derived).** For a bet at net odds b (b = 100/A for -A, A/100 for +A) with P(win) = w, P(push) = u, P(lose) = l: EV per unit = w*b - l; maximising w*ln(1 + b f) + l*ln(1 - f) gives f* = (w*b - l) / (b*(w + l)). Equivalently, use ordinary Kelly with the push-free probability w/(w + l). Example at -110 on a whole number: w = 0.53, u = 0.036, l = 0.434 gives EV +4.8 %, f* = 5.5 % (push-free win probability 0.550).
- **Kelly at -110 (my calc).** True p of 53 / 54 / 55 / 56 / 57 %: full Kelly 1.3 / 3.4 / 5.5 / 7.6 / 9.7 % of bankroll; ROI per bet +1.2 / +3.1 / +5.0 / +6.9 / +8.8 %; per-bet SD of profit about 0.95 units.
- **Shrinkage for uncertain probabilities (derived, second-order).** Betting k times Kelly when the probability estimate has variance var(p) around the truth maximises expected log growth at k* of about s^2 / (s^2 + ((b+1)/b)^2 * var(p)), where s is the Kelly stake. This reproduces both numbers reported from Baker & McHale's Figure 1 (0.5 at SD 0.20 and about 0.94 at SD 0.05 for b = 1, p = 0.7), but the paper's own closed form was not read. At -110: p-hat = 0.55 with SE 0.02 gives k about 0.63; SE 0.03 gives 0.43; SE 0.04 gives 0.30; p-hat = 0.57 with SE 0.03 gives 0.70. Because NBA spread edges are 2-5 points of probability and model SEs are of the same size, **quarter- to half-Kelly is the mathematically indicated range, and bets whose edge is smaller than the SE of the probability get almost no stake**. Cap any single game at 1-2 % of bankroll until the blend weight in section 5 is established.
- **Sample size to prove an edge from results at -110 (my calc; break-even p0 = 0.5238).** Bets needed for the *expected* observed win rate to sit 2 SE above break-even, and for a one-sided 5 % test with 80 % power:

| True win rate | ROI | 2-SE (expected) | 5 % one-sided, 80 % power |
|---|---|---|---|
| 53 % | +1.2 % | 26,000 | 40,200 |
| 54 % | +3.1 % | 3,800 | 5,900 |
| 55 % | +5.0 % | 1,450 | 2,250 |
| 56 % | +6.9 % | 760 | 1,170 |
| 57 % | +8.8 % | 470 | 720 |
| 58 % | +10.7 % | 320 | 490 |

  A full NBA season offers about 1,230 games; a selective model betting 20-30 % of sides and totals places about 500-750 bets a season, so a true 55 % bettor needs two to four seasons of results, and a 54 % bettor five or more. Results alone cannot validate the project quickly.
- **Using CLV instead.** If per-bet CLV has an SD near 0.1 (Buchdahl's soccer figure; not measured for NBA), an average CLV of 2 % is 2 SE from zero after about 100 bets and 1 % after about 400. But positive CLV is not profit: the price taken must beat the *no-vig* close. Relative to a vigged -110/-110 close that means about 4.8 % better decimal odds (the full overround, consistent with Whelan's ninth-decile result), or 10 cents of a 20-cent line (the "half the hold" rule), or about 0.65-0.8 points of spread, or about 1.0-1.1 points of total. Track CLV in points and in no-vig probability for every bet; it is the only edge metric that can become significant within one season.
- **Correlated same-game bets.** A moneyline and a spread on the same team are almost perfectly positively correlated: treat them as one position and solve the three-state problem (team loses / wins but fails to cover / covers) by maximising the sum over states of P(state)*ln(1 + sum of f_i * payoff_i(state)); as a floor, never stake more on the pair than the larger single Kelly stake. Side and total on the same game are approximately independent under the equal-variance bivariate-normal view in section 1, so they can be sized separately with a per-game cap, pending an empirical check of the correlation between spread and total residuals in the project's own data. For a slate of independent games with stakes of 1-3 % each, individual (shrunk) Kelly stakes are close to the joint optimum.
- **Order of validation that the numbers above imply.** (1) Calibration of residual SDs and the margin-to-win map (hundreds of games); (2) blend-weight regression against bet-time and closing lines (thousands of games, can use history); (3) live CLV in points (100-400 bets); (4) realised profit (thousands of bets) as the last and slowest confirmation.

### Gaps
- No NBA-specific measurement of the per-bet SD of closing-line value (in points or in odds) for spreads, totals or moneylines was found, so the "100-400 bets" figure rests on a soccer value.
- No source giving optimal Kelly stakes for same-game combinations of spread, moneyline and total (or the empirical correlation between NBA spread and total results) was found; the treatment above is derived, not cited.
- Baker & McHale's exact closed-form shrinkage estimator, Thorp's sports-betting Kelly paper and Buchdahl's t-statistic / sample-size formulas (football-data.co.uk, Pinnacle resources) could not be retrieved in the original; the sample-size table is plain binomial arithmetic.
- Whether closing lines at DraftKings and Fanatics specifically (rather than a multi-book consensus or a sharp book) are an adequate CLV benchmark was not addressed by any source found.
