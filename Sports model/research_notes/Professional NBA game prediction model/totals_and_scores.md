# NBA totals and team scores: possession-based projection, luck vs skill, overtime, situational effects, scoring environment, market accuracy, joint score modelling

Notes compiled 19 November 2026 (2026-27 season about four weeks old). Conventions used below:
- "B-Ref" = Basketball Reference definitions (box-score possession estimate). "NBA.com" = the league site's own possession count, which runs about 1% more possessions and about 1 point per 100 lower efficiency for the same games. Never mix the two scales.
- "(search extract)" = the page blocked a direct read (403/redirect/paywall), so the figure comes from the search engine's extract of that page. Treat as lower confidence.
- "(my calculation)" items in Cited Findings are arithmetic on the cited numbers and are repeated or expanded under Inferences.

## 1. The standard possession-based method (possessions, pace, combining offence with defence, baselines, turning it into a score and total)

### Takeaway
Team points = game possessions x points per possession, with possessions estimated from box scores (B-Ref formula is the most accurate free option) and pace projected additively from each team's deviation from league pace; offence and opposing defence are also combined additively around the league average (the choice KenPom moved to in 2016 after years of multiplicative). In the NBA's narrow range of paces and efficiencies the additive and multiplicative versions differ by under 0.3 points per team, so the possession definition, the league baseline and overtime handling matter more than that choice.

### Cited Findings
- B-Ref possession estimate: Poss = 0.5 x ((Tm FGA + 0.4 x Tm FTA - 1.07 x (Tm ORB / (Tm ORB + Opp DRB)) x (Tm FGA - Tm FG) + Tm TOV) + (Opp FGA + 0.4 x Opp FTA - 1.07 x (Opp ORB / (Opp ORB + Tm DRB)) x (Opp FGA - Opp FG) + Opp TOV)). Both teams get the same possession count by construction. — [Basketball Reference glossary](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/glossary.html)
- B-Ref pace: Pace = 48 x ((Tm Poss + Opp Poss) / (2 x (Tm MP / 5))), i.e. possessions per 48 minutes, so it is already normalised for overtime. Team ORtg = points scored per 100 possessions, DRtg = points allowed per 100 possessions. — [Basketball Reference glossary](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/glossary.html)
- NBA.com and ESPN use the simple form (FGA + 0.44 x FTA - ORB + TOV) / 2 over both teams' totals; it ignores team rebounds, overestimates possessions, and therefore understates efficiency. B-Ref's formula was the closest match to play-by-play counts for 2014 and 2015. Kevin Pelton's variant multiplies the simple form by 0.96. — [Nylon Calculus 101: Possessions](https://fansided.com/2015/12/21/nylon-calculus-101-possessions)
- Measured error against counted play-by-play possessions, 2016-17 season: the NBA box formula (0.436 x FTA) overestimated by 7,739 possessions over the season (6.29 per game as stated); the B-Ref formula overestimated by 2,189 (1.77 per game); refitting the two weights to 2016-17 data gave 0.4328 on FTA and 1.2228 on the offensive-rebound term with near-zero aggregate error. The author's advice: with box scores only, refit the weights regularly. — [Squared Statistics, "Analyzing NBA Possession Models"](https://squared2020.com/2017/07/10/analyzing-nba-possession-models/)
- Size of the definitional gap in practice: NBA.com reported 2025-26 league averages of 114.7 points per 100 possessions and 100.2 possessions per 48 on 6 April 2026, while B-Ref's full-season 2025-26 figures are ORtg 115.7 and pace 99.4. — [NBA.com Power Rankings, Week 25 2025-26](https://www.nba.com/news/power-rankings-2025-26-week-25); [Basketball Reference league averages](https://www.basketball-reference.com/leagues/NBA_stats_per_game.html)
- Projecting game pace, additive form: Pomeroy's worked example uses a 70-possession average; a team eight possessions slower than average against one two slower gives an expected 60. — [KenPom, "National efficiency"](https://kenpom.com/blog/national-efficiency/) (search extract; old post, college)
- Combining offence and defence: KenPom's October 2016 methodology update replaced the multiplicative/Pythagorean system (expected offensive efficiency as the product of team offence and opponent defence relative to average, win probability via log5) with an additive one (AdjEM = AdjO - AdjD in points per 100 possessions); the stated reasoning is that basketball is probably closer to additive than multiplicative, and log5 win probabilities were dropped. — [KenPom, "Ratings methodology update"](https://kenpom.com/blog/ratings-methodology/) (search extract; college)
- Pace is set mostly by each team's own offence, not by the opponent: over 12,000+ regular-season games (ten seasons to 2015-16) the correlation between the home and away team's seconds per possession was 0.046; team offence explained 39.1% of the variance in seconds per possession, team defence 7.2%, both together 43.2%. The fitted model is additive: intercept 14.88 s per possession, plus an offence effect (Warriors -1.30, Cavaliers +0.79) plus an opposing-defence effect (+0.07, +0.18). — [Inpredictable, "Is pace contagious?"](https://www.inpredictable.com/2016/05/is-pace-contagious.html)
- 2014-15 team ranges: offence 13.4 to 16.2 seconds per possession (2.8 s spread), defence 14.7 to 15.7 (a 1.0 s spread, about a third of the offensive spread); offence 0.93 to 1.10 points per possession, defence 1.00 to 1.09. — [Inpredictable, "Offensive versus Defensive Pace in the NBA"](https://www.inpredictable.com/2015/03/offensive-versus-defensive-pace-in-nba.html)
- League baselines (B-Ref scale): 2025-26 pace 99.4, ORtg 115.7, 115.6 points per team per game; 2026-27 through 222 games (19 Nov 2026) pace 98.8, ORtg 114.4, 113.7 points. Team minutes per game were 241.3 in 2023-24 to 2025-26, i.e. the average game lasts about 48.26 minutes once overtime is included. — [Basketball Reference league averages](https://www.basketball-reference.com/leagues/NBA_stats_per_game.html)
- A 2024 student model for totals stabilised pace by averaging the two teams' cumulative paces and used team offensive/defensive rating and pace features from the NBA API; it roughly matched but did not beat the sportsbook total (details in section 6). — [Carnegie Mellon capstone, "Betting the Buzzer" (2024)](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2024/460files/team6.pdf)

### Inferences
- Working recipe (all on one possession scale, B-Ref recommended because it needs only team box scores):
  - Expected pace per 48: P = Lg + (Pace_home - Lg) + (Pace_away - Lg). Multiplicative alternative P = Pace_home x Pace_away / Lg. Example with Lg 99.4 and teams at 102 and 103: additive 105.6, multiplicative 105.7. The difference is negligible.
  - Expected efficiency: E_home = ORtg_home + DRtg_away - Lg (+ half the home edge in points per 100); E_away = ORtg_away + DRtg_home - Lg (- half the home edge). Multiplicative alternative ORtg x DRtg / Lg. Example with Lg 115.7: offence 122 against defence 120 gives 126.3 additive and 126.5 multiplicative; offence 120 against defence 110 gives 114.3 and 114.1.
  - Regulation points: Pts_team = P x E_team / 100. Total = sum, margin = difference. Then add overtime (section 3).
  - Check on the identity: 2025-26 pace 99.4 x ORtg 115.7 / 100 = 115.0 regulation-equivalent points; times 241.3/240 minutes = 115.6, exactly the published points per game. Pace x efficiency therefore gives a 48-minute score and must be scaled up about 0.5% (about 1.1 to 1.2 points on the total) to match results that include overtime.
- Because the opponent's defence explains less than a fifth as much of the variance in possession length as the team's own offence (and the spread across defences is only about 35 to 45% of the spread across offences), a better pace model than "average the two team paces" is to estimate each team's offensive seconds per possession and defensive seconds per possession separately and add them (needs play-by-play or possession-time data); with box scores only, the additive team-pace form above is the practical substitute.
- For rate statistics bounded between 0 and 1 (the Four Factors), the odds-ratio ("log5") combination is the standard alternative: p = (a x d / L) / (a x d / L + (1 - a)(1 - d) / (1 - L)), where a is the offence's rate, d the defence's allowed rate and L the league rate. It only differs materially from the additive form at extreme rates; KenPom's move away from log5 for efficiencies suggests starting additive and testing.
- Home advantage should be applied as plus and minus half the margin effect to the two efficiencies, which leaves the total unchanged; whether home games also raise totals needs testing on own data.

### Gaps
- Dean Oliver's "Basketball on Paper" is not freely readable and KenPom's blog blocks direct fetches, so the matchup formulas above rest on glossary pages, search extracts and practitioner explainers rather than the primary texts.
- I found no published NBA-specific out-of-sample test of additive versus multiplicative (or log5) combination for pace or efficiency. It needs a backtest on own data.
- NBA.com does not publish its possession definition; the formula above is a reverse-engineered description from 2015 and the gap to B-Ref should be re-measured each season.

## 2. Four Factors: do components beat efficiency-only, what is skill and what is luck, stabilisation points

### Takeaway
The Four Factors explain almost all of team results in-sample (R-squared 0.86 to 0.96 for wins) with shooting carrying 40 to 70% of the weight, but I found no published out-of-sample evidence that a component model predicts games better than opponent-adjusted efficiency. The documented value of components is luck adjustment: opponent 3-point % is close to pure noise (year-to-year r 0.22; best regressed with about 6,000 attempts of league-average shooting), opponent free-throw % is treated as pure luck, and a team's own 3-point % is only partly skill (year-to-year r 0.43; a shooter needs about 750 attempts for reliability 0.7).

### Cited Findings
- Definitions and Oliver's weights: eFG% = (FG + 0.5 x 3P) / FGA; TOV% = TOV / (FGA + 0.44 x FTA + TOV); ORB% = ORB / (ORB + Opp DRB); free-throw factor = FT / FGA; weights roughly 40% shooting, 25% turnovers, 20% rebounding, 15% free throws; eight factors once offence and defence are both counted. — [Basketball Reference, Four Factors](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/factors.html)
- Alternative weightings (shooting / turnovers / rebounding / free throws): regression on point differential (EvanZ, 2010) 54 / 22 / 15 / 10 with only 4% of point differential unexplained; win-probability-added attribution over the 2011-2014 seasons 71 / 12 / 9 / 8. — [Inpredictable, "How NBA Games Are Won"](https://www.inpredictable.com/2015/02/how-nba-games-are-won.html)
- Single-season regression of wins on the eight factors (2016-17): R-squared 0.914; implied weights 46 / 35 / 12 / 7. — [Squared Statistics, "Introduction to Oliver's Four Factors"](https://squared2020.com/2017/09/05/introduction-to-olivers-four-factors/)
- In-sample fit for wins rose from adjusted R-squared 0.86 (2004-05) to 0.96 (2024-25); in the 2025 playoffs (through Game 3 of the conference finals) it was 0.83. No out-of-sample test reported. — [Charting Hoops, "Revisiting the Four Factors"](https://chartinghoops.substack.com/p/revisiting-the-four-factors)
- Year-to-year team correlations, seasons from 1973-74: defensive rebounding (opponent ORB%) 0.79, ORB% 0.72, TOV% 0.70, opponent TOV% 0.70, eFG% 0.69, opponent eFG% 0.66, opponent FT rate 0.63, FT rate 0.62. Shooting components, seasons after 1996-97: 2P% 0.63, opponent 2P% 0.62, own 3P% 0.43, opponent 3P% 0.22. FT% 0.58, FTA/FGA 0.63. — [Basketball Reference blog, "Year-to-Year Four Factor Correlations"](https://www.basketball-reference.com/blog/index8e05.html?p=3475)
- Within-season split-half (first 41 games against last 41; ten seasons from 2012-13 excluding the two shortened ones; 300 team-seasons): eFG% is the most stable factor on both offence and defence; offensive factors are slightly more stable than defensive ones; the exception is free-throw rate, where avoiding fouls is more stable than drawing them. The coefficients appear only in chart images. — [Sebastian Darius, "Measuring the Stability of the Four Factors"](https://sebasdarius.substack.com/p/measuring-the-stability-of-the-four)
- Opponent 3P% is noise: not stable season to season or across 10-game subsets. Best predictive form found: Predicted opponent 3P% = (3PA x observed opp 3P% + 6000 x expected 3P%) / (3PA + 6000) + 0.26 x (opp 2P% - league 2P%), with the prior weight ranging 4,000 to 7,000 attempts depending on seasons used (data 2005 to 2017-18). Adjustments reach 1 to 2 points per game at the extremes. Free-throw rate and other defensive components had weak effects. — [Nylon Calculus, "3-point team defense closure"](https://fansided.com/2018/01/29/nylon-calculus-3-point-team-defense-closure)
- Individual 3P% stabilisation: Kuder-Richardson (KR-21) reliability crosses 0.7 at roughly 750 attempts (seven seasons of NBA.com player data; published August 2014). — [Nylon Calculus, "How Long Does It Take For Three Point Shooting To Stabilize?"](https://fansided.com/2014/08/29/long-take-three-point-shooting-stabilize); a secondary post describes the same 750 figure as the "50% signal, 50% noise" point and guesses free throws stabilise near 100 attempts or fewer without computing it — [Sportstribution](https://sportstribution.blogspot.com/2015/01/revisiting-stats-stabilization.html)
- Luck-adjusted ratings in practice: opponent 3P% and opponent FT% are treated as luck (replaced by the opponent average), teammates' FT% as luck, teammates' 3P% as partly skill (blended with season rates by attempts). Player-level adjustments reached about 10 points per 100 possessions in 2017-18. No predictive validation is reported. — [Nylon Calculus, "Calculating luck-adjusted ratings"](https://fansided.com/2018/01/08/nylon-calculus-calculating-luck-adjusted-ratings)
- How fast team ratings settle: a 2014 analysis found team ratings within about 5% of final-season values after 10 games and about 4% after 18 games — [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2295486-when-do-we-know-who-an-nba-team-actually-is) (search extract). A five-man lineup needs about 550 possessions for offensive rating and about 850 for defensive rating to stabilise (Kostya Medvedovsky's estimate) — [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2023/04/13/nba/2023-nba-playoff-preview-how-to-use-and-not-use-lineup-data) (search extract).
- Volume context for the noise calculations below: 2025-26 teams averaged 37.0 3PA at .360 and 23.5 FTA at .783 per game. — [Basketball Reference league averages](https://www.basketball-reference.com/leagues/NBA_stats_per_game.html)

### Inferences
- Weight on a team's observed opponent 3P% under the 6,000-attempt prior at about 37 opponent attempts per game: 6% after 10 games, 11% after 20, 20% after 41, 34% after 82. Even a full season of opponent 3P% is about two-thirds noise.
- Pure binomial noise in 3P% converted to points (37 attempts, 3 points each): one standard deviation is about 2.5 percentage points = 2.8 points per game over 10 games, 1.8 percentage points = 2.0 points per game over 20 games, and 0.9 percentage points = 1.0 point per game over 82 games. This is the size of the error an unadjusted defensive (or offensive) rating carries from shooting variance alone.
- The same calculation for opponent FT% (23.5 attempts): about 0.6 points per game over 10 games and 0.2 over a season. Worth adjusting but an order of magnitude less important than threes.
- Rough implied priors from the year-to-year correlations (reliability r = n / (n + k), with roughly 1,250 to 1,400 team 3PA per season in that older sample): k of about 1,700 to 1,900 attempts for a team's own 3P% and about 4,300 to 4,800 for opponent 3P%, consistent with the directly fitted 4,000 to 7,000. Roster turnover depresses year-to-year correlations, so the own-3P% prior is an upper bound. Re-fit on 2018-19 onward data.
- Practical component design from free box scores: keep stable pieces near face value (2P%, 3PA rate, TOV%, ORB%/DRB%, FT rate, especially the defensive foul rate), regress own 3P% moderately, regress opponent 3P% and opponent FT% almost fully to league average, then rebuild efficiency as points = 2 x eFG% x FGA + FT% x FTA over the estimated possessions. This is equivalent to a luck-adjusted efficiency rating and should be compared out-of-sample with plain opponent-adjusted efficiency before adopting.
- Since eFG% dominates every weighting and is also the factor most contaminated by 3-point variance, most of the available gain from components is in how the 3-point part is regressed, not in modelling turnovers, rebounds and free throws separately.

### Gaps
- No published out-of-sample comparison of Four-Factor-based versus efficiency-only game projection was found (the BBall Index article "Is Net Rating Still King?" could not be read). This is the key question to settle by backtest.
- Nothing usable from Cleaning the Glass, pbpstats or Positive Residual on luck adjustment was reachable (paid or blocked).
- No team-level stabilisation points in games for the 2018-19 onward era were found in text form; the one split-half study publishes its coefficients only as images.
- The 750-attempt figure is reported against two different thresholds (reliability 0.7 in the original, 0.5 in a secondary summary).

## 3. Overtime: frequency, extra points, handling in totals

### Takeaway
From 2018-19 to 2025-26 about 5.3% of regular-season games went to overtime (4.4% to 6.5% by season, and only about 4.7% over the last three seasons), with 12% of those going to a second overtime or more. Each 5-minute period adds roughly 20 to 22 combined points (about 9.7% of the regulation total), so overtime adds about 1.0 to 1.2 points to the unconditional expected total and a long right tail; since totals bets include overtime, model regulation and add an explicit overtime term.

### Inferences
- Points per overtime period in the current environment: 9.7% of a regulation total of about 225 is about 22; the double-minus-single overtime gap in the 2018-26 lists is 17.3; single-overtime games finish 19.4 above the league average. A working value of 20 to 22 combined points per period (10 to 11 per team) is consistent with all three. The comparison with the league average is biased slightly low because games tied late in regulation have less end-of-game fouling than games being closed out.
- Expected overtime periods per game: 0.0527 x 1.13 = 0.060 for 2018-26 and 193 / 3,690 = 0.052 for the last three seasons. Cross-check from minutes: (241.3 - 240) / 25 = 0.052. Expected addition to the mean total: about 1.1 to 1.3 points.
- Recommended handling: (1) fit and project regulation scores (period line scores are in free box-score data; or use per-48 pace x efficiency, which is overtime-neutral); (2) P(OT) for the game = base rate scaled by closeness, e.g. P0 x exp(-m^2 / (2 x sd^2)) where m is the projected margin and sd about 12 to 13, with P0 near 5.5 to 6% so the league average comes out at about 4.7 to 5.3%; (3) total distribution = (1 - P(OT)) x regulation distribution + P(OT) x (regulation distribution shifted by about 21 per period, with about 12% chance of each further period). The observed tie rate is far above what a smooth normal margin implies, so P0 must be calibrated empirically, not derived from the normal density.
- Overtime mainly matters for the tails (alternate totals, team totals) and for the correlation between the two team scores (both teams gain about 10 points together). It moves the fair main total by about a point, which is half of a typical edge threshold.
- The recent decline in overtime frequency (4.4% to 4.9% in the last three seasons) coincides with record average margins, which is what the closeness logic predicts; use recent seasons for the base rate.

### Gaps
- No published table of overtime probability by closing spread was found, and no play-by-play measurement of overtime scoring for 2018-19 onward; both are easy to compute from free game logs with period scores.
- The per-season counts above rely on a third-party list read by an automated extractor; for three seasons (2019-20, 2020-21, 2021-22) the count was not cross-checked against a page-stated total. Verify against own game data.
- DraftKings and Fanatics house rules on overtime for totals were not checked here (the assignment states bets include overtime).
- Playoff overtime rates were not collected.

## 4. Measured situational effects on margin and total

### Takeaway
The second night of a back-to-back is the only schedule effect measured consistently: about -1.2 to -1.9 points of margin (2004 to 2014 studies) and -1.5 net rating per 100 possessions in 2025-26, made up of slightly worse offence, worse defence and about one fewer possession, which implies a total roughly 1 to 2 points lower. Three-in-four, rest beyond one day and raw travel distance show little or no additional effect; eastward jet lag costs a home team about 1.3 points; altitude is worth roughly 1.5 points of extra home edge in Denver; playoffs cut pace about 2.5% and efficiency by 1 to 2 per 100 in normal years (over 4 in the 2026 first round). For early starts, the NBA Cup, tanking and referee crews I found no reliable measured effect on totals.

### Cited Findings

Back-to-backs and rest days
- 2004-05 and 2005-06, 2,415 games, margin regression with team strengths: 0 days rest versus 3+ days -1.77 points (SE 0.83, p = 0.03); 1 day -0.13 (SE 0.79); 2 days +0.32 (SE 0.85); the effect is the same for home and road teams (F-test p = 0.41). Home teams were on zero rest in 15% of games and visitors in 33%. Rest disparity explains 0.31 of the 3.24-point home advantage (10.5%, 95% CI 5.2% to 17.7%). Second game of a road trip: +1.04 for the home team (p = 0.07); longer trips no effect. Odds of winning on a back-to-back multiplied by 0.75 (95% CI 0.53 to 1.06). — [Entine and Small, "The Role of Rest in the NBA Home-Court Advantage"](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Nba.pdf)
- 2006 to 2014 (eight seasons), margin model with team strengths: back-to-back -1.87 points (SE 0.23); home advantage 2.70 (SE 0.12); for 2013-14 alone -1.85 (SE 0.66) and 2.29 (SE 0.35). Residual variance of margin about 130 to 137 (SD about 11.5); normality not rejected within seasons. — [Manner, "Modeling and forecasting the outcomes of NBA basketball games" (JQAS 2016, author copy)](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- 2003 to 2010, results against closing spreads: zero-rest penalty estimated at 1.25 points (the market had been pricing about 0.75). Home rested versus road on zero rest: average line 4.4, actual margin 4.8 (1,887 games); home on zero rest versus road rested: line 2.1, margin 0.9 (485 games); both rested: line 3.1, margin 2.8 (4,795); both on zero rest: line 3.1, margin 3.3 (681). No additional adjustment could be found for two games in three days or three games in four days. — [Inpredictable, "NBA Home Court Advantage and Rest"](https://www.inpredictable.com/2012/02/nba-home-court-advantage-and-rest.html)
- 2014-15, zero rest versus one or more days: offensive rating 103.4 to 102.3, defensive rating 102.7 to 104.3, net +0.6 to -2.0, almost one fewer possession per game, FG% 44.5 to 44.2, 2.2 fewer points in the paint, fast-break points 13.4 to 12.3. Rest was not a significant factor in shot accuracy once shot quality was controlled. — [Nylon Calculus, "Some Analysis of Game-to-Game Fatigue"](https://fansided.com/2016/01/13/some-analysis-of-game-to-game-fatigue)
- 2016-17, 1,230 games: paint points 42.2 on a back-to-back, 43.6 after one day, 44.6 after two or more (p = 0.001); points from threes 28.7, 28.9, 29.6 (p = 0.011). Not adjusted for opponent or venue. — [Yang et al., Frontiers in Psychology 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7925613)
- 2025-26: teams won about 44% on the second night of a back-to-back against 51% with standard rest; net rating fell by 1.5 points per 100 possessions on average (median 1.2), and 12 of 30 teams were slightly better without rest. Over five years second-night records improve through the season: .437 in the first third, .446 in the middle, .456 in the final third, but lottery teams in the final third were 91-211 (.301). — [Stacker / SBD analysis of Sportradar data](https://stacker.com/stories/basketball/surviving-schedule-how-back-back-games-will-hurt-or-help-nba-teams-2026-27)
- FiveThirtyEight's 2015-16 model docked a team that played the previous day about 46 Elo points (about 5 percentage points of win probability), cancelling when both teams did; it used a home bonus of about 92 points, a travel penalty linear in miles from the previous game, and an altitude bonus linear in arena elevation worth about 47 points for Denver; 100 Elo points equal about 3.5 NBA points. — [FiveThirtyEight, "How Our 2015-16 NBA Predictions Work"](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-our-2015-16-nba-predictions-work) and [FiveThirtyEight, "How We Calculate NBA Elo Ratings"](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/how-we-calculate-nba-elo-ratings/) (search extracts; the site now redirects to ABC News)

Travel distance and time zones
- 2013 to 2020, 3,005 back-to-back games: win rates by sequence were 54.4% away-then-home, 39.2% away-away, 36.8% home-then-away; when returning home, each additional 500 km lowered the likelihood of winning by about 4% (p = 0.038); distance had no effect for away-away (p = 0.37) or home-away (p = 0.31); eastward travel 44.5% wins against 40.8% westward. No point-differential results reported. — [Charest et al., J Clin Sleep Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8636381)
- 2011-12 to 2020-21, 11,481 games: home teams with eastward jet lag had a point differential of +1.24 against +2.53 with no jet lag (difference -1.29, p = 0.015) and won 54.6% against 58.1%; two hours of eastward jet lag: -2.81 average differential (adjusted -4.53 against one hour); westward jet lag showed no effect (+2.91); no significant effects for away teams. — [Leota et al., Frontiers in Physiology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.892681/full)

- 2014 to 2018, 9,840 games: travel distance mattered more than circadian misalignment for point differential, and the second game of a back-to-back was the robust predictor of disadvantage. — [Chronobiology International 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/07420528.2022.2113093) (search extract)

### Inferences
- Suggested starting priors (to be re-estimated on 2018-19 onward game logs; "team" effects are for the affected team's margin):

| Factor | Margin effect (points) | Total effect (points) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second night of back-to-back, opponent rested | -1.5 (range -1.2 to -1.9) | about -1 to -2 | margin measured; total inferred from one fewer possession (about -2.3) plus offence -1.1 and defence +1.6 per 100 (about +0.5) |
| Both teams on back-to-back | 0 | about -2 to -3 | cancels in margin; total is an extrapolation, weak |
| 3-in-4, 4-in-6 beyond the back-to-back itself | 0 | 0 | no additional effect detectable in 2003-10 data; four-in-fives no longer scheduled |
| 1 versus 2 versus 3+ days of rest | 0 | 0 | coefficients -0.13 and +0.32, not significant |
| Home team with eastward jet lag (back from the west) | -1.3 | unknown | Leota et al.; nothing for away teams |
| Long trip home on a back-to-back | small negative, on top of the back-to-back term | unknown | Charest et al. (win likelihood only) |
| Altitude: Denver home games | +1.5 extra home edge (Utah about +1.3 if linear in elevation from sea level) | unknown | 47 Elo points at 3.5 points per 100 Elo |
| Playoffs | handled by team strengths | pace -2.5%, efficiency -1 to -2 per 100 in 2021-22 to 2024-25; larger in 2026 | Ringer, TSN, Action Network |
| Referee crew | 0 | at most about +/-1 after shrinkage | see next point |
| Early start, NBA Cup group game, late-season tanking | no measured effect | no measured effect | gaps |

- Conversion used for FiveThirtyEight's numbers: 46 Elo x 3.5 / 100 = 1.6 points for a back-to-back and 47 Elo = 1.6 points for Denver's altitude, both in line with the regression studies.
- Referee spread versus noise: with a per-game total SD near 19 and about 55 to 60 games per referee, the standard error of a referee's average total is about 2.5 points, so among several dozen referees extremes of about +/-6 are expected by chance alone; the observed range (-8.0 to +5.9) is barely wider. A single referee is also only one of three on the floor. Any crew adjustment should be shrunk by 80 to 90%, or built from the crew's regressed foul rate rather than raw totals.
- The back-to-back effect on totals is small and already in the line (in 2003-10 the average closing line already moved about 1.0 to 1.3 points for a one-sided back-to-back), so it is a correctness term for the model, not an edge.
- Tanking and resting are better handled through expected lineups and minutes than through calendar dummies; the measurable trace in the data is that lottery teams' second-night results collapse late in the season.
- Playoff totals need a separate pace and efficiency baseline; a regular-season model will project several points too high (2.8 points per team historically, far more in 2026).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate for 2018-19 onward of the back-to-back, three-in-four or rest-advantage effect on totals (or even on margin in points) was found; NBA.com's annual schedule articles did not include league-wide efficiency splits.
- No point estimate of altitude in peer-reviewed work; no measured effect of tip-off time on regular-season totals; no Cup-only over/under record or scoring comparison; no measurement of late-season totals for eliminated or resting teams; no significance test of referee effects on totals.
- Roy and Forest (2018) on westward travel and evening games was only available as press summaries without numbers.
- All of these can be estimated from free game logs by regressing margin and total residuals (actual minus model or minus closing line) on indicator variables.

## 5. League scoring environment by season, drift within a season, 2025-26 and 2026-27 changes

### Takeaway
On B-Ref's scale the league has sat at 114 to 116 points per 100 possessions and 98.5 to 100 possessions per 48 since 2022-23, with 3-point attempt rate still creeping up (39.5% in 2023-24, 42.2% in 2024-25, 41.5% in 2025-26, about 43% so far in 2026-27). The unstable element is whistles: free throws collapsed after February 2024 (about 4 points per team per game), rebounded in 2025-26 and are higher again early in 2026-27 alongside new offensive-foul points of emphasis, with efficiency so far 1.3 lower than last season. Early-season drift is real but not consistent in direction, so the league baseline should be a rolling estimate rather than a constant.

### Inferences
- Within-season drift, summarised: (a) free throws and fouls are highest in the first week and decline (about 1.5 attempts per team on average, about 5 in 2024-25); (b) pace declines by roughly 1 to 1.5 possessions from the opening weeks to the full season (101.6 to 100.2 on NBA.com's scale in 2025-26; 101.1 to 96.9 over five weeks in 2021-22); (c) efficiency rose through the season in the 2016 to 2021 era (+2.6 per 100 after the first two weeks) but was flat in 2025-26 (114.6 to 114.7). Net effect on scoring differs by year: early 2025-26 scoring (117.7) was about 2 points per team above the final 115.6, while 2021-22 started far below the market.
- A fixed prior-season league baseline can therefore be wrong by 3 to 4 points of total in October and November in either direction. Use a league-level state (rolling or exponentially weighted pace and efficiency, starting from last season's values and updating daily) and express team ratings relative to it.
- 2026-27 so far against 2025-26: efficiency -1.3 per 100 (B-Ref) or about -2.2 on NBA.com's scale at the same calendar date, eFG% -0.9 points, turnovers +0.8 per 100 plays, offensive rebounding +1.3 points, fouls +2.0 per team, free-throw rate +.013. More fouls with lower efficiency and more turnovers is the signature of offensive fouls, consistent with the push-off and illegal-screen emphasis, but the causal link is unverified and four weeks is a small sample.
- Record margins (13.8 average) mean the spread of margins is widening while overtime frequency is falling; margin and total variance parameters should be estimated on the last two or three seasons rather than a long history.
- Two published 3-point-rate figures for early 2026-27 disagree (42.1% through 28 October on NBA.com, about 43.3% implied by B-Ref's per-game columns through 19 November); the dates differ and the B-Ref figure is computed from rounded numbers.

### Gaps
- No month-by-month league efficiency and pace series for 2022-23 onward was found in a citable source; it must be computed from game logs.
- All 2026-27 figures are from the first four weeks and will be out of date quickly.
- Whether the 2026-27 changes have lowered totals relative to market expectations (over/under record to date) was not found.

## 6. Accuracy of sportsbook closing totals, accuracy of public and academic models, documented biases

### Takeaway
The sportsbook total misses the final combined score with a standard deviation of about 17 to 18 points (MSE 321 in 2021-22 and 332 in 2022-23; about 17.1 to 17.4 in 2007-12), equivalent to a mean absolute error of roughly 13 to 14.5; public and student models report MAE of about 14 to 14.4 and RMSE of about 18 to 18.3, statistically indistinguishable from the line and no better. Documented biases (overs overbet, unders profitable at the highest totals, early-season totals mispriced) come mostly from pre-2010 data and later work finds the market more efficient.

### Cited Findings
- Sportsbook ("Vegas") total against final total: MSE 320.98 in 2021-22 and 332.08 in 2022-23. The same paper's models: linear regressions 334.6 to 335.9 in cross-validation on 2021-22 and 332.1 to 336.7 on 2022-23; a multilevel model with team effects 325.8 and 324.5; none of the differences significant; a claimed 56.5% betting simulation result on one season. — [Carnegie Mellon capstone, "Betting the Buzzer" (2024)](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2024/460files/team6.pdf)
- Linear regression of total points on exponentially smoothed box-score statistics: R-squared 0.22, MAE 14.36; adding the opening total improved MAE to 13.95; judged insufficient to beat the books; a neural network and a Gaussian process gave similar MAE. — [Carnegie Mellon capstone poster (2020)](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/capstoneresearch/spring2020/495-Peterson-Poster.pdf) (search extract; the PDF text is not machine-readable)
- Practitioner claims (anecdotal, December 2024): bookmaker totals MAE about 13.4, a hobbyist model at 14.4, closing-total MAE about 13 over a season, and an asserted floor of about 12 for any model. — [r/algobetting thread](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/algobetting/comments/1hbezkg/nba_total_score_predicting) (search extract)
- 2007-08 to 2011-12: actual totals had mean 198.7 and SD 20.55; actual home margins mean 3.09 and SD 13.20; the Gaussian noise needed to turn the bookmaker distribution into the actual one was SD 17.12 for totals and 11.565 for spreads; over and home-cover frequencies were near 50%. — [Points in the Paint, "Accuracy and Precision of Bookmaker Point Spreads and Totals"](http://pts-in-the-paint.blogspot.com/2012/05/accuracy-and-precision-of-bookmaker.html)
- Forum estimates from 2009-10: SD 17.4 points for totals lined 200 to 209.5, with 1.5 points on the total worth moving a +100 bet to 53.4%; raw SD of game totals 18.3 across the league (team range 15.6 to 20.6); points scored and points allowed each averaged 100.3 with SD 11.1. — [SBR forum, "Which is the std dev for NBA totals?"](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/handicapper-think-tank/349745-which-is-the-std-dev-for-nba-totals)
- Variance rises only slightly with the size of the total (correlation 0.33, not significant; totals above 200 had nearly a point more spread than those below). — [Boyd's Bets](https://www.boydsbets.com/standard-deviations-of-overunder-margins-by-total/)
- Point spreads for comparison, 2006 to 2014 out-of-sample (second half of each regular season): opening spread MSE 137.5 and MAE 9.17; a team-strength model with home and back-to-back terms MSE 142.2 and MAE 9.36; an equal-weight combination 137.9; playoffs: spread 149.0 and 9.50. In-sample residual variance about 130. — [Manner (2016)](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- 2004 to 2010, NFL, NBA and college: the spread is an accurate predictor of margin but the over/under is a poor predictor of the sum of scores. — [Kain and Logan, Journal of Sports Economics 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v15y2014i1p45-63.html)
- 10,320 games, 2000-01 to 2007-08: average posted total 192.27 against average final 192.72; overs 5,059, unders 5,086, pushes 175. — [Crown Wagers summary of Compton and Sigler (The Sport Journal, 2012)](https://www.crownwagers.com/nba-deviation-for-game-totals/)
- 22,804 games since the 2008 season against the opening total: overs 11,231, unders 11,294, pushes 279 (49.9% overs). — [TeamRankings](https://www.teamrankings.com/nba/odds-movement/over-under/results/)
- Over rate by opening total (seasons not stated; the same long database): 51.9% at 185.5 or lower (908 games), 51.7% at 186 to 190.5 (1,300), 50.3% at 191 to 195.5 (1,867), 49.3% at 196 to 200.5 (2,042), 49.6% at 201 to 205.5 (2,121), 50.5% at 206 or higher (14,941). Within 206+, when the line fell 2 to 2.5 points from open to close the over went 780-676 (53.6%); with moves of 3+ either way results were 49.5% and 50.8%. — [BetIQ / TeamRankings](https://betiq.teamrankings.com/nba/betting-trends/over-under-results-by-opening-line-and-line-movement/)
- Academic bias findings: 1995-96 to 2001-02, market efficient overall but not at the highest totals — [Paul, Weinbach and Wilson (2004)](https://econbiz.de/Record/efficient-markets-fair-bets-and-profitability-in-nba-totals-1995-96-to-2001-02-paul-rodney/10007646715); re-test on 2012-13 to 2018-19 finds a more efficient market, the fair-bet null not rejected for high totals, with win rates above 52.4% only in the far upper tail — [Moore (2021)](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:quaeco:v:82:y:2021:i:c:p:26-29); overs attract a much higher share of bets, rising with each point of the total, yet betting against the public does not beat efficiency — [Paul and Weinbach (2008)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/jsf/intjsf/v3y2008i3p137-145.html) (search extract); totals lines are significantly biased early in each season and a simple strategy against the closing total won 56.72%, while sides showed no such bias — [Baryla, Borghesi, Dare and Dennis (2007)](https://dc.etsu.edu/etsu-works/17780)
- Playoff totals: a betting write-up puts historical playoff unders at 53 to 54%. — [Action Network](https://www.actionnetwork.com/nba/nba-playoffs-over-under-betting-trends-matchup-analysis-for-every-round-1-series) (search extract; low confidence)

### Inferences
- RMSE implied by the 2021-23 MSEs: 17.9 and 18.2 points. For normal errors MAE = 0.80 x SD, so an SD of 18 corresponds to an MAE of 14.4; reported MAEs of 13 to 13.4 imply an SD nearer 16.5. A working value for the SD of the final total around a market-quality forecast is 17 to 18.5 points in the current scoring environment (the 2007-12 figure of 17.1 at a mean total of 199 scales to about 18.3 at 228 if variance is proportional to the mean).
- Benchmarks for the project: a model built from free data that reaches RMSE of about 18.3 or MAE of about 14.3 on totals is at par with published student models; the closing line is only about 0.2 to 0.5 MAE better. Differences of that size need several seasons to detect (one season of 1,230 games gives a standard error on MSE of roughly 13, as the cross-validation spread of about 30 on 246-game folds shows).
- Value of a point: near the centre of a normal with SD 18, one point on the total is worth about 2.2 percentage points of win probability (the 2010 forum figure is 3.4 points per 1.5 points). Beating -110 pricing (52.4% break-even) therefore needs a true edge of more than about 1.1 points against the posted number.
- Because the line's error is almost all irreducible game variance, evaluate the model on closing-line value and on error relative to the line, not on raw over/under win rate.
- The documented biases worth testing on recent data are narrow: early-season totals (the market was 3.9 points too high over the first five weeks of 2021-22), the highest totals, and the playoffs. Treat the long-run TeamRankings splits as noise unless they replicate season by season.

### Gaps
- No season-by-season series of closing-total error (SD, RMSE or MAE) for 2018-19 through 2025-26 was found in a citable source, and nothing specific to DraftKings or Fanatics. It requires an odds archive; free historical closing lines are patchy.
- The direction and duration of the early-season totals bias in Baryla et al. is not stated in the abstract; Kain and Logan's NBA-specific statistics were not accessible.
- No peer-reviewed NBA totals model with out-of-sample accuracy against closing lines for the current era was found; Unabated and Action Network analytics pieces were not reachable.

## 7. Modelling the two team scores jointly; correlation between them; joint distributions

### Takeaway
The two scores should be modelled jointly: they are positively correlated (0.25 in a 2024-25 sample; about 0.36 to 0.42 implied by 2007-12 dispersion data, and about 0.37 after conditioning on the market) because both teams play the same number of possessions and share game-level conditions such as overtime and officiating. Independent team models therefore understate the variance of the total and overstate the variance of the margin; a bivariate normal for (home, away) with a separate overtime mixture is the usual practical choice.

### Cited Findings
- Home and away points had a correlation of 0.25 in a 2024-25 season dataset (student project). — [INST414 Medium post, "Home Court Advantage in NBA"](https://medium.com/inst414-data-science-tech/home-court-advantage-in-nba-ff7d2bdd4ea8) (search extract; page blocked, not peer-reviewed)
- Dispersion data that pin down the correlation: 2007-08 to 2011-12 actual totals SD 20.55 and margins SD 13.20; noise around the bookmaker's total SD 17.12 and around the spread SD 11.565. — [Points in the Paint](http://pts-in-the-paint.blogspot.com/2012/05/accuracy-and-precision-of-bookmaker.html)
- 2009-10: team points for SD 11.1, points against SD 11.1, game totals SD 18.3. — [SBR forum](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/handicapper-think-tank/349745-which-is-the-std-dev-for-nba-totals)
- Both teams are assigned the same possession count in the standard estimate (the formula averages the two teams' estimates). — [Basketball Reference glossary](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/glossary.html)
- Time per possession is not shared: home and away seconds per possession correlate only 0.046, so the common factor is the possession count and game length, not a mutual speeding up. — [Inpredictable, "Is pace contagious?"](https://www.inpredictable.com/2016/05/is-pace-contagious.html)
- Margin residuals are close to normal: normality is not rejected within single seasons (2006 to 2014), there is weak evidence of team-specific variance, residual variance is about 130 to 137. — [Manner (2016)](https://static.uni-graz.at/fileadmin/_Persoenliche_Webseite/manner_hans/Publikationen/NBA_revision2.pdf)
- Joint estimation with correlated team effects across outcomes (a normal or Poisson score response together with a binary win response) improved prediction accuracy in college football and college basketball. — [Broatch and Karl, "Multivariate Generalized Linear Mixed Models for Joint Estimation of Sporting Outcomes"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05284)
- Overtime adds to both teams at once: overtime games finish about 20 points above the average total with a final margin averaging 4.7 points (2018-19 to 2025-26, my calculation from the season lists). — [Land of Basketball, e.g. 2024-25 list](https://www.landofbasketball.com/game_records_by_year/2025_most_overtimes_rs.htm)
- Margins are getting wider: 13.8-point average winning margin early in 2026-27, a record. — [AP via theScore](https://www.thescore.com/nba/news/3652201)

### Inferences
- With equal team variances s^2 and correlation r: Var(total) = 2 s^2 (1 + r) and Var(margin) = 2 s^2 (1 - r), so r = (Var_total - Var_margin) / (Var_total + Var_margin) and s^2 = (Var_total + Var_margin) / 4.
  - Raw scores 2007-12 (SD 20.55 and 13.20): r = 0.42, team SD 12.2.
  - Around market expectations 2007-12 (SD 17.12 and 11.565): r = 0.37, team SD 10.3. This is the number a simulation needs: the correlation of the two teams' forecast errors.
  - Raw scores 2009-10 (team SD 11.1, total SD 18.3): r = 0.36 and an implied margin SD of 12.6.
  - Current era, if the total's SD is 18 and the margin's is 13: r = 0.31, team SD 11.1; with a margin SD of 12, r = 0.38. The modern margin SD against the spread was not sourced and should be measured.
- Consequence of ignoring the correlation: with team SD 11 and r = 0, the total's SD would be 15.6 instead of about 18 and the margin's 15.6 instead of about 13. That misprices totals tails, spreads, moneylines and any same-game combination.
- Two equivalent practical parameterisations: (a) bivariate normal on (home, away) with means from section 1, team SD about 11 and r about 0.3 to 0.4; (b) independent normals on (total, margin) with SD about 18 and about 13, which is the same model when team variances are equal. Either way add the overtime mixture from section 3 rather than inflating r to absorb it, and discretise to integers to get push probabilities on whole-number lines.
- A structural alternative that produces the correlation by construction: draw the game's possessions once (shared), then draw each team's points given possessions. The implied shared component (r x s^2 of about 40, SD about 6.5 points per team) is larger than plausible pace variation alone, so overtime, foul-heavy games, end-game fouling and garbage time must also be contributing; fit the residual correlation empirically instead of assuming it all comes from pace.
- Team-specific variances are weakly supported; a single league-wide covariance matrix, re-estimated on the last two or three seasons, is a reasonable start.
- Recipe to estimate everything from free data: for each game compute residuals (home points minus projection, away points minus projection), then their two variances and correlation, by season and split by regulation-only and final scores.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or practitioner estimate of the home/away score correlation for 2018-19 onward was found beyond one unverified student figure (0.25); the 0.3 to 0.4 range is derived from older dispersion data.
- No published NBA comparison of joint versus separate score models for totals accuracy, and no published copula or bivariate count model for NBA scores, was found.
- The standard deviation of margin against the closing spread for recent seasons (needed for the modern correlation) was not found.
